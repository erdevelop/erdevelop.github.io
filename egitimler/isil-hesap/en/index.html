Thermal Calculation for Facades

A step-by-step thermal calculation from profile section to curtain wall module: 2D model, Uf and psi, Uw and Ucw, condensation check and report, with worked examples in 7 days.

DAY 01

Basics

~3.5 h · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the basics of heat transfer (λ, R, U, Rsi, Rse), the names of the U-values used for facades (Ug, Uf, Up, Uw, Ucw, ψ), the standards behind them and where national requirements fit in. About 3 hours.

About this course: a step-by-step thermal calculation for facade and window sections: U-values by hand, Uw with EN ISO 10077-1, 2D numerical frame calculation (Uf, ψ) with EN ISO 10077-2, curtain walls with EN ISO 12631, and condensation checks. Background: Facade Physics (in Turkish). Values in the examples are for teaching; for an official calculation use the current editions of the standards and the project specification. Terms are in the glossary.

1. Basic concepts (1 hour)

Symbol Meaning Unit
λ (lambda) Thermal conductivity of the material W/(m·K)
d Thickness m
R = d / λ Thermal resistance of a layer m²·K/W
Rsi, Rse Internal and external surface resistances m²·K/W
U = 1 / Rtotal Thermal transmittance (heat through 1 m² per 1 K difference) W/(m²·K)
ψ (psi) Linear thermal transmittance (extra loss per 1 m of length) W/(m·K)

For a plain layer: U = 1 / (Rsi + Σ d/λ + Rse). For horizontal heat flow (walls, facades) the usual values are Rsi = 0.13 and Rse = 0.04 m²·K/W.

2. Example: spandrel panel (45 min)

100 mm mineral wool (λ = 0.035) and thin metal sheets (their resistance is ignored):

U = 1 / (0.13 + 0.10 / 0.035 + 0.04) = 1 / (0.13 + 2.857 + 0.04) = 1 / 3.027 = 0.33 W/(m²·K)

This is the value at the centre of the panel. The aluminium frames and connections at the edges are thermal bridges; the real value of the facade is calculated together with them (day 6).

3. U-values on a facade (45 min)

Symbol Of what How it is found
Ug Centre of glass EN 673 (from the glass manufacturer)
Up Centre of the opaque panel By hand (as above)
Uf Frame profile EN ISO 10077-2 numerical calculation or test (day 4)
ψg, ψp Frame-glass and frame-panel junctions EN ISO 10077-2 numerical calculation (day 5)
Uw Window EN ISO 10077-1 (day 2)
Ucw Curtain wall EN ISO 12631 (day 6)

4. National requirements (15 min)

Limit values for facade components come from national rules (for example TS 825 in Turkey, the national energy regulations in EU countries). Find which climate zone your project is in and what the code requires for windows and curtain walls.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 02

Uw

~3.5 h · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will be able to calculate a window's Uw value with EN ISO 10077-1 from areas, the glazing perimeter and ψ. About 3 hours.

1. Formula (45 min)

Uw = (Ag·Ug + Af·Uf + lg·ψg) / (Ag + Af)

Symbol Meaning
Ag Visible glass area
Af Frame area (projected)
lg Visible glass perimeter
ψg Extra loss where the glass edge (spacer) meets the frame

Areas and lengths are taken from the visible sizes (whichever of the inside or outside is larger); check the measuring rules of the standard.

2. Example (1 hour)

Window 1230 × 1480 mm with a 100 mm visible frame on every side; Ug = 1.1, Uf = 2.0 W/(m²·K), ψg = 0.06 W/(m·K):

Item Calculation Value
Aw 1.23 × 1.48 1.8204 m²
Ag 1.03 × 1.28 1.3184 m²
Af Aw − Ag 0.5020 m²
lg 2 × (1.03 + 1.28) 4.62 m
Ag·Ug 1.3184 × 1.1 1.4502 W/K
Af·Uf 0.5020 × 2.0 1.0040 W/K
lg·ψg 4.62 × 0.06 0.2772 W/K
Uw 2.7314 / 1.8204 1.50 W/(m²·K)

3. What matters most? (45 min)

Change New Uw
Ug 1.1 → 0.7 (triple glazing) (0.9229 + 1.004 + 0.2772) / 1.8204 = 1.21
Uf 2.0 → 1.4 (1.4502 + 0.7028 + 0.2772) / 1.8204 = 1.33
ψg 0.06 → 0.035 (warm edge spacer) (1.4502 + 1.004 + 0.1617) / 1.8204 = 1.44

In small windows the frame and edge effects grow; in large glazing Ug dominates.

4. Tables or calculation? (15 min)

EN ISO 10077-1 also gives table values for Uf and ψ; they are on the safe (pessimistic) side. Better values for a real profile require a 2D numerical calculation (days 3-5) or a test.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 03

2D model

~3.5 h · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will be able to model a profile section for a 2D numerical thermal calculation: geometry, materials, air cavities and boundary conditions. About 3.5 hours.

1. Why 2D? (30 min)

In a profile section, aluminium, the thermal break (polyamide), gaskets and air cavities sit side by side; heat finds the easiest path. That is why Uf and ψ are calculated with a 2D finite element model of the section (EN ISO 10077-2). The standard also gives example sections for validating software (validation cases); check that your program solves them correctly.

2. Geometry (45 min)

  1. Take the section as DXF (from the system supplier's catalogue or your own drawing).
  2. Simplify unnecessary detail: screw holes, very small fillets.
  3. Turn every region into a closed area (polygon) with no overlaps and no gaps.

3. Materials (45 min)

Typical thermal conductivities (W/(m·K); take exact values from the standard or the manufacturer):

Material λ
Aluminium alloy 160
Steel 50
Stainless steel 17
Polyamide with 25 % glass fibre (thermal break) 0.30
EPDM gasket 0.25
Silicone 0.35
Glass 1.0
Insulation panel (for the Uf calculation) 0.035

Aluminium conducts about 530 times better than polyamide: the width and continuity of the thermal break decide Uf.

4. Air cavities (45 min)

  1. Closed cavities: modelled as a solid with an equivalent thermal conductivity given by the method in the standard, depending on the cavity size and temperature difference (convection and radiation included).
  2. Slightly ventilated cavities (with small openings to the outside) and ventilated cavities are treated separately in the standard.
  3. The program may do this automatically, but you decide which cavity is which type.

5. Boundary conditions (30 min)

Surface Temperature Surface resistance
Inside θi = 20 °C Rsi = 0.13 m²·K/W (normal)
Inside, corners and recesses 20 °C Rsi = 0.20 m²·K/W (where radiation and convection are reduced)
Outside θe = 0 °C Rse = 0.04 m²·K/W
Cut edges — Adiabatic (no heat flow)

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 04

Uf

~3.5 h · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will be able to calculate Uf according to EN ISO 10077-2: insulation panel instead of glass, 2D heat flow, subtracting the panel contribution. About 3 hours.

1. Method (1 hour)

  1. Remove the real glass; put an insulation panel of the same thickness (λ = 0.035) in its place.
  2. The visible width of the panel must be bp ≥ 190 mm (so that the edge effect dies out).
  3. Solve the model → Lf2D: the total heat through the section per 1 K temperature difference (W/(m·K)).
  4. Subtract the panel's contribution:

Uf = (Lf2D − Up · bp) / bf

Symbol Meaning
Lf2D Thermal coupling coefficient from the 2D calculation
Up U-value of the insulation panel (by hand)
bp Visible panel width
bf Visible (projected) frame width

2. Example (1 hour)

28 mm panel, bp = 0.19 m, bf = 0.08 m, Lf2D = 0.40 W/(m·K) from the program:

Step Calculation Result
Up 1 / (0.13 + 0.028 / 0.035 + 0.04) = 1 / 0.97 1.031 W/(m²·K)
Up · bp 1.031 × 0.19 0.196 W/(m·K)
Lf2D − Up·bp 0.40 − 0.196 0.204 W/(m·K)
Uf 0.204 / 0.08 2.55 W/(m²·K)

3. Checks (45 min)

  1. Temperature distribution (isotherms): they should crowd in the thermal break; the inner and outer aluminium parts should each be at almost one temperature.
  2. Heat flux: look where the flux arrows "escape": screws, continuous aluminium bridges, badly modelled cavities.
  3. Mesh: if refining the mesh no longer changes the result, it is fine enough.
  4. Compare the value with catalogue values of similar profiles.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 05

ψg

~3.5 h · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will be able to calculate the ψg value of the frame-glass junction: model with real glass and spacer, subtracting the Uf and Ug contributions. About 3 hours.

1. Method (1 hour)

  1. This time put the real insulating glass unit in the same section: glass panes, spacer, secondary seal (butyl, silicone/polysulphide), gas fill.
  2. Visible glass width again bg ≥ 190 mm.
  3. Solve → L2Dψ.
  4. ψg = L2Dψ − Uf · bf − Ug · bg

Uf comes from day 4; Ug from the glass manufacturer's EN 673 value.

2. Example (45 min)

Uf = 2.55, bf = 0.08 m, Ug = 1.1, bg = 0.19 m, L2Dψ = 0.48 W/(m·K) from the program:

Step Calculation Result
Uf · bf 2.55 × 0.08 0.204
Ug · bg 1.1 × 0.19 0.209
ψg 0.48 − 0.204 − 0.209 0.067 W/(m·K)

3. Spacers (45 min)

Spacer ψg tendency
Aluminium High
Stainless steel Medium
Warm edge (polymer, composite) Low

ψg also affects the internal surface temperature at the glass edge: in cold climates an aluminium spacer increases the condensation risk at the glass edge (day 6).

4. Opaque panel junction (15 min)

The same method gives ψp for the spandrel panel junction; only the real panel section is modelled instead of glass.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 06

Ucw and condensation

~3.5 h · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will be able to calculate a curtain wall module's Ucw with the component method and check condensation from the internal surface temperature. About 3.5 hours.

1. Ucw: component method (1.5 hours)

The component method of EN ISO 12631:

Ucw = (ΣAg·Ug + ΣAp·Up + ΣAf·Uf + Σlg·ψg + Σlp·ψp) / Acw

Example module 1500 × 3200 mm (Acw = 4.80 m²); areas and lengths are examples:

Component Quantity U / ψ Contribution (W/K)
Glass Ag 3.20 m² 1.1 3.520
Panel Ap 1.10 m² 0.33 0.363
Frame Af 0.50 m² 2.2 1.100
Glass edge lg 7.6 m 0.067 0.509
Panel edge lp 4.0 m 0.15 0.600
Total 6.092
Ucw 6.092 / 4.80 1.27 W/(m²·K)

Check: Ag + Ap + Af = 3.20 + 1.10 + 0.50 = 4.80 m² ✓.

Note: although the centre of the panel is 0.33, the module average is much higher because of the edges and the frame. Reporting opaque areas as "U = 0.33" is misleading.

2. Condensation and fRsi (1.5 hours)

In cold weather, if the coldest point of the internal surface falls below the dew point, condensation forms. At 20 °C and 50 % relative humidity the dew point is ≈ 9.3 °C.

Temperature factor:

fRsi = (θsi,min − θe) / (θi − θe)

Example: θi = 20 °C, θe = −5 °C, coldest internal surface from the 2D model θsi,min = 13 °C:

fRsi = (13 − (−5)) / (20 − (−5)) = 18 / 25 = 0.72

An example criterion (e.g. DIN 4108-2 in Germany) is fRsi ≥ 0.70; this section passes. Use the criterion in the project specification. For condensation checks a larger internal surface resistance such as Rsi = 0.25 is commonly used (effect of curtains, furniture); check your standard.

3. Where is it cold? (15 min)

The coldest points are usually: the glass edge (spacer), mullion-transom junctions, anchors and brackets (3D bridges) and the slab edge. A 2D model does not fully capture corners and point bridges; critical places may need a 3D calculation.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 07

Report

~3.5 h · 0/10 checks

Goal

On the last day you will look at tools, reporting, common mistakes and the final exercise. About 3.5 hours.

1. Tools (45 min)

Tool Note
THERM (LBNL) Free, widely used 2D thermal bridge program
Flixo Commercial; quick models from DXF, 10077-2 and 12631 reports
Other finite element programs Check that they solve the validation cases
Excel 10077-1 and 12631 area calculations (days 2 and 6)

Whatever program you use, solve the standard's validation cases once and keep the results: they show in the report that the program is validated.

2. Report (1 hour)

A thermal calculation report includes:

  1. Project, section name, profile series, revision.
  2. Standards and editions (EN ISO 10077-1/-2, EN ISO 12631, EN 673).
  3. Geometry (section drawing), materials and λ values (with sources).
  4. Cavity modelling and boundary conditions.
  5. Results: Lf2D, Uf, L2Dψ, ψg, isotherm and heat flux images.
  6. Uw / Ucw calculation and comparison with the code or specification.
  7. Condensation assessment (fRsi).

3. Common mistakes (30 min)

Mistake Result
bp or bg < 190 mm Edge effect does not die out; Uf/ψ wrong
Solving Uf with real glass Uf and ψ get mixed
Filling cavities with solid aluminium Uf far too high
Drawing screws and point connections as continuous in 2D Uf too high; the 3D effect must be handled separately
Using Rsi = 0.13 everywhere Internal surface looks warmer than it is at corners
Measuring visible widths wrongly Uw/Ucw wrong
