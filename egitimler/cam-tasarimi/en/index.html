Glass Design and Structural Basics

A 7-day, diagram-rich programme for everyone who selects glass, reviews its calculation or orders it in façade and architectural projects. From glass types to plate calculation, from laminated and insulating glass behaviour to thermal breakage, from setting blocks to the glass specification. By the end you can roughly check a glass thickness and write a complete glass type specification.

Before you start

Who is it for

Façade designers, architects, technical teams of glass and façade companies. For background, see Façade Design Basics and Façade Physics Calculations (Turkish).

What you need

  • A calculator or Excel.
  • A glass manufacturer's product catalogue and calculation software make it easier.

DAY 01

Glass and strength

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know how glass is made, the strength differences between glass types, why glass behaves "brittle" and why edge work matters. About 4 hours.

About this course: It explains glass selection and the logic of thickness calculation with diagrams and worked examples. The numbers in the examples are typical values chosen for teaching. In a real project glass structural design is done by a qualified engineer to EN 16612, EN 16613 and the related product standards. For background see Façade Design Basics (day 3: glass).

1. Float glass production (1 hour)

  1. Sand, soda, lime and recycled glass are melted at about 1500 °C.
  2. The molten glass is poured onto a bath of molten tin; floating on the tin it forms a flat ribbon with parallel surfaces. Thickness is set by the drawing speed of the ribbon (2-25 mm).
  3. It is cooled slowly in the annealing lehr; internal stresses are relieved. This glass is annealed float glass.
  4. The standard sheet size is 3210 × 6000 mm (jumbo); larger sizes are special production.

The face that touched the tin (tin side) matters for some coating and toughening processes; the manufacturer marks it.

2. Glass types and strength (1.5 hours)

Type How Characteristic bending strength When broken
Float (annealed) Production above ~45 MPa Large, sharp pieces
Heat-strengthened (HS, TVG) Heated and cooled in a controlled way ~70 MPa Large pieces, stays in the frame
Toughened (FT, ESG) Heated and cooled rapidly ~120 MPa Small blunt pieces
Chemically strengthened Ion exchange High Like float

Why is toughened glass stronger? In rapid cooling the surfaces solidify first, then the core shrinks and puts the surfaces into compression. Glass is very strong in compression and weak in tension; breakage always starts from a small flaw on the tension surface. The surface compression is like "armour" that must be overcome before a flaw can open.

Note: Glass strength is not a single number; it depends on load duration and surface flaws (day 2). The values in the table are characteristic; design strength is well below them with safety and duration factors.

3. Edge work (1 hour)

Edge Appearance Use
Cut edge Sharp, rough Only inside a frame, if thermal stress is low
Arrised (seamed) Corners blunted Minimum treatment before toughening
Ground (matt) Flat, matt Common in framed façades
Flat polished Flat, shiny Visible edges, balustrades

Most breakages start at the edge: notches and micro-cracks at the edge cause stress concentrations. The edge is processed before toughening and heat treatment; toughened glass cannot be cut, drilled or edge-worked afterwards.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 02

Loads and supports

~4 hours · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the loads acting on glass, the effect of load duration on glass strength and the glass support types. Before calculating, you will be able to answer "how is the glass held, what does it carry". About 4 hours.

1. Loads on glass (1.5 hours)

Load Time Note
Wind Very short (seconds) Pressure and suction; usually governing for vertical glass
Self-weight Permanent Causes bending in sloped and horizontal glass; in vertical glass it rests on setting blocks
Snow Weeks On sloped and overhead glass
Barrier load (balustrade) Short Horizontal line load, impact
Climatic load (insulating glass) Hours-days Temperature and pressure change of the gas in the cavity (day 4)
Thermal stress Hours Part of the glass is hot, the edge cold (day 5)

2. Load duration and glass strength (1 hour)

Micro-cracks on the glass surface grow slowly under load, also helped by moisture (stress corrosion). That is why the same glass carries short-term load much better than long-term load.

Load duration Relative strength (rough)
Wind gust (seconds) 1
Snow (weeks) ~0.6
Permanent (weight) ~0.4

In EN 16612 this effect is accounted for with the kmod factor. Result: a sloped overhead glass is thicker than a vertical glass of the same size, because its self-weight and snow are permanent and long-term loads. In toughened and heat-strengthened glass the surface pre-compression largely reduces this effect.

3. Support types (1.5 hours)

Support How it works Note
Four-edge supported Glass sits in the frame on all four edges Most efficient; most curtain walls
Two-edge supported Only two opposite edges Glass works like a one-way beam; thicker glass
Structural silicone Edges bonded with silicone Wind suction carried by silicone; weight usually by setting blocks
Point-fixed (spider, rotule) With drilled fittings Stress concentration around the hole; toughened or heat-strengthened laminated mandatory
Clamped With clamps at the edge Balustrades and interior glazing

The more "continuous" the support, the thinner the glass can be. For a four-edge supported 1.4 × 2.8 m pane 10 mm may be enough (day 3), but the same pane supported on two edges must be clearly thicker.

DAY 03

Thickness calculation

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will be able to roughly calculate the stress and deflection under wind of a single four-edge supported (monolithic) pane with plate tables and choose a thickness. About 4 hours.

1. Plate formulas (1.5 hours)

A rectangular plate simply supported on four edges, under uniform load:

Stress      σ = β × q × b² / t²
Deflection  w = α × q × b⁴ / (E × t³)

q: load (N/mm²)   b: short edge   a: long edge   t: glass thickness   E ≈ 70 000 N/mm²

β and α depend on the aspect ratio (a/b) (linear plate theory, approximate values):

a/b β α
1.0 0.287 0.044
1.5 0.487 0.084
2.0 0.610 0.111
3.0 0.713 0.134

Square panes are the most efficient; as the aspect ratio grows the glass starts to work like a beam across its short edge.

2. Worked example (1.5 hours)

Glass 1400 × 2800 mm (a/b = 2), wind 1.2 kN/m² = 0.0012 N/mm².

         σ = 0.610 × 0.0012 × 1400² / t²      w = 0.111 × 0.0012 × 1400⁴ / (70 000 × t³)

t = 8 mm  → σ ≈ 22.4 MPa   w ≈ 14.2 mm
t = 10 mm → σ ≈ 14.4 MPa   w ≈ 7.3 mm
t = 12 mm → σ ≈ 10.0 MPa   w ≈ 4.2 mm

Rough acceptance values for comparison (for short-term load such as wind, at the level of the old German TRLV rules): float ~18 MPa, heat-strengthened ~29 MPa, toughened ~50 MPa.

  • 8 mm float: 22.4 > 18 → not enough. 8 mm toughened: 22.4 < 50 → enough.
  • 10 mm float: 14.4 < 18 → enough.
  • Deflection: Project limit e.g. 1/60 of the short edge: 1400 / 60 ≈ 23 mm. All three thicknesses pass.

3. Large deflection and reading the results (45 min)

When deflection exceeds half the glass thickness (in 10 mm glass 7.3 > 5 mm) the glass starts carrying part of the load by membrane action and the real stress is lower than the linear formula gives. The linear calculation then stays on the safe side; finite element programs (non-linear analysis) give a more economical result.

Rule: A hand calculation is for checking whether the thickness is of the right order. The final decision is the calculation by the glass manufacturer or engineer to EN 16612.

End-of-day check (15 min)

Exercise: A 1500 × 1500 square pane, same load, 8 mm float: σ and w? (Answer: β = 0.287, σ ≈ 12.1 MPa; α = 0.044, w ≈ 7.5 mm.)

DAY 04

Laminated and insulating glass

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know how laminated glass works, the interlayer types and the effective thickness concept; how load is shared between the two panes of insulating glass and climatic loads. About 4 hours.

1. Laminated glass (1.5 hours)

Two or more panes are bonded with a plastic interlayer between them under heat and pressure.

Interlayer Property
PVB Most common; flexible, good sound insulation (acoustic PVB even better); softens as temperature and load duration increase
SGP (ionoplast) Much stiffer and stronger; superior post-breakage behaviour; balustrades, overhead and walk-on glass
EVA Good moisture resistance; decorative and interior applications

Effective thickness: The more the interlayer prevents slip between the panes, the more the laminated glass behaves as one piece.

Layered (free slip, worst):   t_ef = ³√(Σ tᵢ³)
Fully bonded (best):          t_ef = Σ tᵢ

6 + 6 laminated:  layered ≈ 7.6 mm    fully bonded = 12 mm

Real behaviour lies in between and depends on the interlayer's shear stiffness, load duration and temperature (EN 16613). PVB is stiffer in short-term wind and in the cold, softer under long-term load and in the heat.

Post-breakage: The real value of laminated glass shows after it breaks: fragments stay stuck to the interlayer and do not fall. In overhead glass, balustrades and wherever people pass below at height, the inner pane or single pane must be laminated.

2. Load sharing in insulating glass (1.5 hours)

Thanks to the sealed gas between them, the two panes of an insulating glass unit carry the load together. The load is shared roughly by their stiffness (cube of the thickness):

Outer pane share = t₁³ / (t₁³ + t₂³)

8 + 6 IGU:  8³ = 512   6³ = 216   →  outer pane 70%, inner pane 30%

So each pane must be checked for its own share; the thinner pane takes less load but, being thinner, its stress may still govern. The share of a laminated pane is calculated with its effective thickness.

3. Climatic loads (45 min)

Insulating glass is sealed at the temperature and air pressure of the factory. At the site:

  1. If temperature rises the gas expands and the panes bulge outward.
  2. If air pressure drops (high-altitude site, storm) the gas again pushes outward.
  3. Altitude difference: Insulating glass made at the coast and moved to a high-altitude city bulges permanently.

The effect is large on small and narrow panes: large panes flex and balance the pressure, small panes are stiff and stress stays high. A wide cavity increases the effect. In small units (e.g. 400 × 800 mm) climatic load may govern over wind.

DAY 05

Thermal breakage and special glass

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the causes of thermal breakage and risky situations, the special rules for balustrade, overhead and walk-on glass, and the nickel sulphide risk. About 4 hours.

1. Thermal stress and thermal breakage (1.5 hours)

The sun heats the centre of the glass; the edge inside the frame stays shaded and cold. The centre wants to expand and stretches the edge. If the tensile stress at the edge exceeds the strength, the glass breaks from the edge with a crack perpendicular to it.

Glass Rough acceptable temperature difference
Float (annealed) ~40 K
Heat-strengthened ~100 K
Toughened ~200 K

What increases the risk:

  1. High absorption: tinted glass, solar control coating, dark ceramic frit (spandrel glass).
  2. Partial shading: canopy, column, neighbouring building shadow; part of the glass hot, part cold.
  3. Heat build-up behind the glass: close internal blinds, insulated spandrel back, unventilated cavity.
  4. Poor edge work.

In these cases heat-strengthened or toughened glass is chosen. Glass manufacturers calculate thermal breakage risk; for spandrel glass this calculation is almost always required.

2. Special uses (1.5 hours)

Use Rule
Balustrade glass Laminated (usually with toughened or heat-strengthened plies); line barrier load and impact test (EN 12600 pendulum); must carry load for a while even when broken
Overhead (sloped/horizontal) glass In insulating glass the inner pane is laminated: broken glass must not fall on people below. Self-weight and snow enter as permanent loads
Walk-on glass Multi-ply laminated (e.g. 3 plies), top ply for wear and slip resistance; must carry load even if one ply breaks
Doors and low-level glass Impact risk: toughened or laminated safety glass

Post-breakage safety is the common question of special uses: "What happens if this glass breaks?" If the answer is "it falls", the design must change.

3. Nickel sulphide and heat soak test (45 min)

Float glass rarely contains nickel sulphide (NiS) crystals. In toughened glass these crystals change volume over time and the glass can break spontaneously without any external cause, sometimes years after installation.

  1. Heat soak test (HST, EN 14179): Toughened panes are held in an oven at a set temperature; most panes containing NiS break in the test and are removed. The risk is not eliminated but greatly reduced.
  2. Heat-strengthened glass is much less sensitive to NiS; this is one reason to choose heat-strengthened instead of toughened in laminated glass.
  3. On tall buildings HST is usually mandatory in the specification for outer toughened panes.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 06

Setting blocks and fixings

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know how glass sits in the frame, setting block layout, edge cover and clearances, and the principles of point and structural silicone fixings. About 4 hours.

1. Setting blocks (1.5 hours)

Setting block: Transfers the glass weight to the transom. Made of hard (shore A 80-90) EPDM, silicone or similar.

  1. Position: At a certain distance from the corners (according to the glass and system guide); two blocks.
  2. Length: According to the glass weight; must fully support both panes of insulating glass.
  3. Compatibility: The block material must be chemically compatible with the IGU silicone and laminate interlayer; incompatible material damages the IGU edge.

Location blocks (spacers/edge blocks): Centre the glass in the frame and stop it moving sideways and up; they carry no load.

In opening vents: Blocks are placed in a diagonal pattern so the sash does not sag: the glass stiffens the sash like a "brace". There is a load-bearing block at the bottom on the hinge side and at the top on the opposite side.

2. Edge cover and clearances (1 hour)

Dimension Meaning Why
Edge cover (bite) The part of the glass inside the frame Glass must not slip out in wind; protects the IGU edge from UV
Edge clearance Between glass edge and the bottom of the rebate Thermal movement, interstorey drift, tolerance
Face clearance Between glass face and profile (gasket space) Gasket compression, sealing

Under interstorey drift the frame racks like a parallelogram; glass corners must not touch the profile. Edge clearance must be enough to accommodate the frame movement by rotation of the glass.

3. Point and silicone fixings (1 hour)

Point (rotule) fixing:

  1. The glass is carried by a fitting through a hole; there is stress concentration around the hole.
  2. The glass must be toughened or heat-strengthened (the hole is drilled before toughening), usually laminated.
  3. An articulated (rotule) head lets the glass rotate; a rigid fitting creates extra stress at the hole edge.
  4. The calculation is done with finite elements; hand formulas are not enough.

Structural silicone (Façade Physics course, day 6): The silicone bite carries wind suction; the glass weight is usually carried by mechanical setting blocks (or calculated silicone). It is not applied without adhesion and compatibility tests.

4. Weight in installation (30 min)

Glass weight ≈ 2.5 kg/m² × total glass thickness (mm). An 8 + 16 + 4.4.2 IGU is ~42 kg/m²; a 1.4 × 3.5 m pane ~205 kg. About 1 kN falls on two setting blocks; vacuum lifting equipment and the installation plan are chosen accordingly. Calculate your own panes with the site's Glass Weight calculator.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 07

Selection and specification

~4 hours · 0/4 checks

Goal

On the last day you turn the week's knowledge into a glass selection process and a glass specification: which question to start with, in what order to decide, what to check in ordering and quality control. About 4 hours.

1. Glass selection flow (1.5 hours)

  1. Location and use: Vertical or sloped? Balustrade? Do people pass below? Height?
  2. Safety: What happens if it breaks? → need for laminated, toughened or heat-strengthened.
  3. Thermal and solar performance: Ug, g, LT targets (Façade Physics) → coating, gas, cavity.
  4. Sound: Rw target → asymmetric IGU, acoustic PVB.
  5. Structure: Wind, weight, snow, climatic load → thicknesses (days 3-4).
  6. Thermal breakage: Tinted glass, shading, spandrel → need for heat treatment (day 5).
  7. Appearance: Colour, reflection, anisotropy (pattern seen in polarised light on toughened glass), print.
  8. Buildability: Maximum size, weight, transport and installation.

2. Glass specification and type definition (1 hour)

A glass type definition must be complete:

CT-01  Vision glass, vertical
Outer : 8 mm heat-strengthened, #2 solar control + low-e coating, ground matt edge
Cavity: 16 mm, 90% argon, black warm edge spacer, silicone secondary seal
Inner : 6.6.2 laminated, heat-strengthened + PVB (0.76 mm)
Performance: Ug ≤ 1.1 W/m²K · g ≈ 0.30 · LT ≈ 60% · Rw ≥ 38 dB
Note  : Thermal breakage calculation, CE marking, IGU to EN 1279

3. Ordering, tolerances and quality (1 hour)

Topic Check
Order list Glass type, size (width × height, which edge first), quantity, mark no., edge work, hole/cut-out drawings
Dimensional tolerances Cutting and toughening tolerances (e.g. ±1-2 mm); step tolerance for stepped IGU edges
Visual quality Acceptable defect limits when viewed from a given distance and angle (related product standard and specification)
Documents CE marking, declaration of performance, HST report, IGU certificate
Delivery Label and mark no., stillage order matching installation order, edge protection

4. Tools on the site (15 min)

  • Glass Weight: insulating and laminated glass weight.
  • The Glass Thickness tool in the special tools section: preliminary thickness estimate.
  • Wind Load: design load on the glass.

End-of-day check (15 min)
