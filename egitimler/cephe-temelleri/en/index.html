Façade Design Basics

A 7-day, diagram-rich programme for newcomers to the façade industry, architects and façade draughtspeople. Each day explains one topic with sections and diagrams. By the end you can read a curtain wall section, follow the path of loads and water, and examine a detail with the right questions.

7 days~4 hours a dayEnglishCode examples can be copied

Before you start

Who is it for

Designers and draughtspeople starting at façade companies, architects working with façades, site and procurement teams. No prior knowledge needed. For the calculation side, this course continues with Façade Physics Calculations (Turkish).

What you need

  • No software is needed; pen and paper are enough.
  • A façade system manufacturer's catalogue (section drawings) makes it easier.
  • The buildings around you: try to spot something on a real façade every day.

DAY 01

Façade and system types

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know what a façade does, the curtain wall types and their differences, and be able to guess which system a building uses by looking at it. About 4 hours.

About this course: Professional knowledge, not software. Each day explains one topic with plenty of diagrams. The numbers given are examples and typical values; in a real project the applicable standards, specification and approved calculation reports govern.

1. What does a façade do? (1 hour)

The façade is the building's skin between outside and inside. It is not part of the structure (it carries its own weight and wind, not the building) but it determines most of the building's performance.

Function What it means
Watertightness Keeping out rain and wind-driven water
Airtightness Preventing uncontrolled air flow (heat loss, dust, sound)
Load bearing Transferring its own weight, wind and maintenance loads to the slab
Thermal insulation Limiting heat loss in winter and heat gain in summer
Sun and light Letting daylight in, preventing overheating and glare
Sound insulation Reducing traffic and city noise
Movement Accommodating building, thermal and wind movements without cracking
Fire Preventing fire and smoke spread between floors
Appearance and maintenance Architectural expression, cleaning and long life

All these functions affect each other: thicker glass is better for sound and heat but increases weight and cost. Façade design is the work of balancing them.

2. Curtain wall types (2 hours)

Curtain wall: a non-load-bearing façade hung from the slabs, usually made of aluminium frames and glass.

Type How it is built Strength Watch out
Stick Mullions and transoms are fixed one by one on site, glass is glazed in place Economical, flexible, low and mid-rise buildings Quality depends on site labour and weather
Unitised Panels are prepared in the factory including glass and lifted by crane Fast installation, factory quality, tall buildings Long design and factory preparation, transport
Semi-unitised Mullions on site, glass modules arrive ready In between Interfaces must be solved well
Structural silicone (SSG) Glass is bonded to the frame with silicone; no cap outside Flat, uninterrupted glass appearance Silicone calculation and testing mandatory
Point-fixed (spider) Glass is fixed to the structure with drilled fittings Very transparent, lobbies and atria Glass and fitting statics are sensitive
Ventilated façade Opaque cladding (composite, ceramic, stone) fixed to a sub-structure, with an air gap behind Insulation outside, little thermal bridging Opaque façade; windows are a separate system
Double skin Ventilated cavity between two façade layers Acoustic and solar control Cost, maintenance, fire

3. Recognising a building (45 min)

  1. Is there a cap (bead) outside at the glass edge? If yes, a pressure plate system (stick or unitised); if not, structural silicone or a hidden frame.
  2. Are there double mullion lines at panel joints? Unitised panels join two half mullions (male-female) vertically; up close the joint is visible.
  3. Is there a horizontal band at floor level? An opaque spandrel band hides the slab edge and the fire stop.
  4. Is the glass held by holes? Point-fixed system.

Exercise: photograph three buildings around you and guess their type; write down why.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 02

Profiles and sections

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will be able to read a curtain wall section and know the position and function of the mullion, transom, thermal break, pressure plate, gasket and setting block. About 4 hours.

1. Stick system horizontal section (1.5 hours)

Taking a horizontal section through the mullion, from inside to outside:

Part Function
Mullion Vertical structural profile. Transfers wind and the glass weight from the transoms to the anchor
Inner gasket Main airtightness line (inner side)
Glass / insulating glass Infill element; its edges are clamped by gaskets
Outer gasket Keeps most of the water outside
Thermal break (insulating spacer) Cuts the thermal bridge while connecting the outer pressure plate to the mullion; polyamide or similar
Pressure plate Clamps the glass to the mullion with screws
Cover cap Covers the pressure plate and screws; the visible outer profile

Sightline: The profile width visible from outside. Typical values 50-65 mm. One of the dimensions architects care about most.

2. Transom and vertical section (1 hour)

  1. Transom: horizontal profile. Carries the glass weight via setting blocks and transfers it to the mullion.
  2. Setting block: hard plastic pieces placed under the bottom edge of the glass. Glass never sits directly on aluminium.
  3. Drainage: Water entering the transom is led down and out through the cavity on the outer side of the transom and through the mullion (day 5).
  4. Transom-mullion joint: The transom is fixed to the mullion with a connector (T-joint); gasketed or sealed watertightness is needed at the ends.

3. Unitised system joints (1.5 hours)

Each unitised panel has its own frame. The mullions of two neighbouring panels interlock as male-female half mullions.

  1. Vertical joint: Two half mullions together form a full mullion. Gaskets in the joint accommodate small movements of the panels relative to each other.
  2. Horizontal joint (stack joint): The bottom transom of the upper panel slides into the top transom of the lower panel. This joint is the movement joint that accommodates slab deflection and thermal movements (day 5).
  3. Installation sequence: Panels are usually installed bottom to top and in one direction; each panel "slides" into its neighbour. The last panel (closing panel) needs a special solution.
  4. Because sealing and insulation are done in the factory, quality is high and wet work on site is minimal.

End-of-day check (15 min)

Exercise: Open one of the section drawings in a system manufacturer's catalogue and mark the parts using today's table.

DAY 03

Glass

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the glass types, the build-up of insulating glass, which surface coatings go on, and be able to read and write a glass specification ("8 toughened + 16 argon + 4.4.2 laminated, #2 low-e"). About 4 hours.

1. Glass types (1.5 hours)

Type How it is made When broken Use
Float (annealed) Molten glass floated on tin Large, sharp pieces Raw material for laminated and processed glass
Heat-strengthened (HS) Heated and cooled in a controlled way Large pieces, stays in the frame In laminated glass, against thermal stress
Toughened (tempered, ESG) Heated and cooled rapidly; ~4-5 times stronger than float Small, blunt pieces Safety, where high strength is needed
Laminated (VSG) PVB/SGP interlayer between two or more panes Fragments stay stuck to the interlayer Fall risk, balustrades, overhead glass, acoustics
  1. Toughened glass cannot be cut or drilled; all processing is done before toughening.
  2. Toughened glass rarely breaks spontaneously due to nickel sulphide (NiS). A heat soak test (HST) is applied to reduce the risk.
  3. Where glass is high up and people pass below, laminated glass, which does not fall when broken, is preferred.

Reading it: 4.4.2 = 4 mm glass + 4 mm glass + 2 × 0.38 mm PVB (0.76 mm interlayer in total).

2. Insulating glass (1.5 hours)

Insulating glass unit (IGU): two or three panes with a dry air or gas-filled cavity between them.

Component Function
Outer pane Weather, wind; usually the coating is here (#2)
Cavity (12-20 mm) Thermal insulation; better with argon gas
Spacer Separates the panes; contains desiccant. "Warm edge" spacers reduce the thermal bridge
Primary seal (butyl) Prevents gas and moisture transfer
Secondary seal (silicone/polysulphide) Holds the panes together mechanically. In a structural silicone façade it must be silicone
Inner pane Usually laminated (safety, acoustics)

Surface numbers: Counted from outside in: the outer face of the outer pane is #1, its inner face #2, the cavity face of the inner pane #3, the room face #4.

3. Coatings and selection (45 min)

Coating Function Typical surface
Low-e (low emissivity) Reflects heat back, lowers the Ug value #2 or #3
Solar control Reflects solar heat, lowers the g value #2
Ceramic print (frit) Pattern, shading, opaque band #2

An example glass specification:

Outer : 8 mm toughened, #2 solar control + low-e coating
Cavity: 16 mm, 90% argon, warm edge spacer
Inner : 4.4.2 laminated (4 + 0.76 PVB + 4)
Total: ~33 mm · Ug ≈ 1.0-1.1 W/m²K · g ≈ 0.30 · LT ≈ 60%

Ug, g and LT values are taken from the glass manufacturer's calculation software (Façade Physics course, days 2 and 5).

DAY 04

Anchors and load path

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know how the façade transfers its loads to the building, the difference between fixed and sliding anchors, why a 3D adjustable bracket is needed and what building tolerances are. About 4 hours.

1. Load path (1 hour)

Every load follows a path until it reaches the building structure:

  1. Glass passes wind and its own weight to the frame at its edges.
  2. Transom takes the glass weight from the setting blocks and passes it to the mullion.
  3. Mullion carries wind and glass weight floor by floor.
  4. Anchor (bracket) connects the mullion to the slab.
  5. Slab and structure take the load down to the foundations.

Every part and connection on the way (screw, bolt, weld, anchor) is verified by calculation. The chain is only as strong as its weakest link.

Load Direction Who carries it
Dead load (weight) Vertical, downward Setting block → transom → mullion → fixed anchor
Wind pressure and suction Perpendicular to the façade, both ways Glass → frame → mullion → all anchors
Maintenance, barrier, impact Horizontal or vertical Relevant profiles and connections
Earthquake Interstorey drift Joints that accommodate movement (day 5)

2. Fixed and sliding anchors (1.5 hours)

A mullion is connected to the slab at every floor. Even if the mullion is continuous over two floors, its weight is hung from a single point:

  1. Fixed anchor (dead load): Carries the mullion's weight and wind. Usually at the top of the mullion; the mullion works hung, i.e. in tension (no buckling risk).
  2. Sliding anchor (wind load): Carries wind only; lets the mullion expand and contract freely in the vertical direction (slotted hole, sliding plate).
  3. At the mullion splice two mullions are joined with a sleeve and an expansion gap is left between them.

Why? Aluminium expands with heat (~6.6 mm for a 3.6 m mullion at an 80 °C difference) and slabs deflect. If a mullion were fixed at two points, these movements would cause stress and noise (ticking).

3. 3D adjustable bracket and tolerances (1.5 hours)

In a concrete building the slab edge may be a few centimetres away from its drawn position. The façade, however, is made to millimetre precision. The adjustable bracket bridges the gap.

Direction How it is adjusted Typical range
Perpendicular to the façade (in-out) Slotted holes of the bracket on the slab ±25 mm
Along the façade (left-right) Sliding in the anchor channel or slotted hole ±25 mm
Vertical (up-down) Adjusting screw or slotted hole + serrated plate ±20 mm
  1. Anchor channel (cast-in channel): A C-shaped steel channel placed in the slab before concreting. T-head bolts can slide along the channel; it gives adjustment along the façade and fast installation.
  2. If the channel was forgotten or is in the wrong place, chemical or mechanical anchors are used afterwards; the anchor calculation and concrete edge distance are checked.
  3. Once adjustment is complete the bracket is locked: serrated plates, welding or a locking screw. An unlocked adjustment slips over time.
  4. Survey: All slab edges are surveyed before installation; if deviations exceed the adjustment range, special brackets are needed.

DAY 05

Water, air and movement

~4 hours · 0/6 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know how water gets into a façade, the rainscreen and pressure equalisation principle, the drainage path and the movements the façade must accommodate. About 4 hours.

1. Why does water get in? (1 hour)

For water to get through a gap two things are needed: an opening and a force that drives water through it.

Force How Measure
Gravity Flows through a gap sloping upward Slope gaps outward
Kinetic energy A wind-driven drop hits the gap Don't open the gap straight out, make a labyrinth
Surface tension Water clings and travels along the surface Break the flow with a drip
Capillarity Sucked into a narrow gap Make the gap wide enough or close it completely
Pressure difference If outside pressure is higher than inside, water is pushed in Pressure equalisation

The first four forces are handled by geometry. The pressure difference is the strongest and needs a special principle.

2. Rainscreen and pressure equalisation (1.5 hours)

Two-stage sealing:

  1. Outer stage (rainscreen): Stops most of the water but is not fully tight; the cavity behind it is connected to the outside air through small openings.
  2. Cavity (pressure equalisation chamber): Connected to the outside air, so its pressure quickly equalises with the outside. With no pressure difference water is not pushed in.
  3. Inner stage (air barrier): The main airtightness line. Wind load is carried here. The inner gasket and corner joints must be fully sealed.

Drainage: The small amount of water passing the outer stage collects in the cavity and is discharged through drainage holes. The holes are usually in the bottom outer face of the transom, hidden by the cap; they must have baffles so wind does not push water in.

Rule: Air barrier inside, water barrier outside. The inner gasket must be continuous around the whole perimeter; continuity at corners and mullion-transom joints is checked carefully.

3. Movements (1.5 hours)

Movement Source Size (example) Accommodated by
Thermal expansion Aluminium heating and cooling ~6.6 mm for a 3.6 m mullion at an 80 K difference Mullion splice gap, sliding anchor
Slab deflection Live load, creep 10-20 mm at the slab edge Stack joint (unitised), mullion splice
Interstorey drift Wind and earthquake ~1/500 of the floor height (~7 mm at 3.6 m) or more Sliding of panel joints, glass clearances
Building settlement and shortening Concrete shrinkage, load Accumulates over floors in tall buildings Joint gaps
  1. Stack joint: The horizontal joint between upper and lower unitised panels. It accommodates both deflection and thermal movement; its design gap must exceed the sum of these movements.
  2. Glass edge clearance: The glass sits in the frame with a few mm clearance on every side; as the frame racks like a parallelogram under drift, the glass corners must not touch the profile.
  3. Movement values are taken from the structural design and specification; the façade designer turns them into joint details.

DAY 06

Fire, interfaces, maintenance

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the façade's role in fire safety, its interfaces with slabs and columns, opening vent types and maintenance and cleaning needs. About 4 hours.

1. Fire: the slab-façade gap (1.5 hours)

The curtain wall passes in front of the slab; a gap of usually 50-200 mm remains between the slab edge and the façade. If it is not closed, fire and smoke pass to the floor above like a chimney.

  1. Fire stop: The gap is tightly filled with non-combustible stone wool and sealed on top with a smoke-tight fire sealant or membrane.
  2. The stone wool sits on a non-combustible support (steel angle or sheet) on the façade side; it must stay in place even if the façade profiles soften in a fire.
  3. Spandrel (opaque band): The opaque zone in front of the slab; insulation and fire-resistant panels may be behind it. Regulations require a certain vertical non-combustible distance to limit flame spread outside between floors.
  4. The fire class (non-combustibility) of materials and the system's tested detail are defined in the specification; they cannot be swapped for a "similar" material.

2. Interfaces (1.5 hours)

The façade does not work alone; every place where it meets another trade is a risk point.

Interface Watch out
Slab Anchor, fire stop, junction with floor finish and skirting
Column Gap between façade and column, fire and sound; column cladding
Roof and parapet Top end of the façade, connection to the roof waterproofing, anchors for maintenance equipment
Ground and entrance Keeping water out of the floor, threshold, doors
Partition walls Junction detail preventing sound and fire transfer
Mechanical and electrical Louvres, lighting, lightning protection connection

Interface matrix: At the start of the project a table of "who does it, who supplies the material, which detail" is prepared for every interface. An undefined interface is where most problems arise on site.

3. Opening vents and maintenance (1 hour)

Vent How it opens Note
Parallel opening Projects outward parallel to the façade plane Does not spoil the curtain wall appearance
Top-hung (opens outward) Bottom edge outward Ventilation; limited opening at height
Bottom-hung (inward) Top edge inward Cleaning and ventilation
Smoke vent Automatic on a fire signal Part of the fire strategy

Maintenance and cleaning: In tall buildings a building maintenance unit (BMU) on the roof or rail systems are planned for façade cleaning. Maintenance anchors and guide rails on the façade are included in the façade design from the start. Glass replacement (how will a broken pane be replaced?) is a question to consider for every system.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 07

Process and testing

~4 hours · 0/4 checks

Goal

On the last day you learn the flow of a façade project from start to finish, the documents produced, performance tests and the mock-up test. About 4 hours.

1. Project stages (1.5 hours)

Stage What is produced Who
Concept Façade type, module, materials, elevation decisions Architect + façade consultant
Preliminary design and specification Performance specification, typical details, system selection Façade consultant
Tender Bids, quantities, alternative systems Contractor, client
Shop drawings All elevations, sections, details, anchor plans, panel lists Façade contractor's design team
Calculation reports Structural, thermal, condensation, glass and silicone calculations Contractor's engineers
Samples and testing Mock-up, laboratory tests Contractor + independent laboratory
Fabrication Fabrication drawings, cutting lists, glass orders Factory
Installation Survey, anchors, panel installation, quality control Site
Handover As-built drawings, maintenance manual, warranties Contractor

Between shop drawings and fabrication there is usually an approval cycle: drawings are reviewed by the consultant and architect, comments are incorporated and resubmitted as a revision.

2. Standards and performance tests (1.5 hours)

The curtain wall product standard in Europe and Turkey is EN 13830. Performance is classified with the related test standards:

Performance What is measured Test standard (EN)
Air permeability Amount of air passing at given pressure differences EN 12153 (test), EN 12152 (class)
Watertightness (static) Whether water penetrates at increasing pressure under spray EN 12155 (test), EN 12154 (class)
Resistance to wind load Deflection and permanent damage under design wind EN 12179 (test), EN 13116 (requirement)
Impact resistance Pendulum impact EN 14019
Thermal performance Ucw EN ISO 12631 (calculation)
Sound insulation Rw EN ISO 10140 (test), EN ISO 717-1

Some projects also require American standards (AAMA/ASTM) and a dynamic water test (wind-driven rain simulated with an aero engine). Which tests are done in which order is written in the specification.

3. Mock-up test (1 hour)

A typical façade section chosen for the project (e.g. 2 floors × 3 modules, with a corner and an opening vent) is built at full size and mounted on the test chamber.

  1. Initial test: Initial measurements for airtightness and water.
  2. Wind pressure: Deflections are measured at design load; they must not exceed the limits.
  3. Repeat air and water: Is performance maintained after the wind load?
  4. Safety load: 1.5 times the design load: no permanent damage and no parts falling off.
  5. If required: Dynamic water, interstorey drift test, impact.

If a leak appears in the test, the cause is found, the detail is fixed and the fix is also carried into the fabrication drawings. The real value of a mock-up is catching detail errors before fabrication starts.

4. Your own checklist (15 min)

Questions to ask when examining a façade detail:

  1. Is the load path continuous, are the fixed and sliding anchors in the right place?
  2. Is the air barrier inside and continuous, is the water barrier outside, where does it drain?
  3. Is the thermal break continuous, is there a thermal bridge?
  4. Where are movements (thermal, deflection, drift) accommodated?
  5. Are the fire stop and slab interface solved?
  6. Are tolerances and the adjustment range sufficient?
  7. How is glass replaced, how is maintenance done?

End-of-day check (15 min)
