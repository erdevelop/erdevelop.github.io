DAY 01

Definition and types

~3.5 h · 0/3 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know what a double skin facade is, why and when it is used, its drawbacks and its basic types. About 3 hours.

About this course: types, ventilation, solar control, fire, acoustics and maintenance of double skin facades (DSF) from a facade designer's point of view. Basic facade physics: Facade Physics (in Turkish); thermal calculation: Thermal Calculation. Example figures are for teaching; every project needs energy and airflow (CFD) analysis. Terms are in the glossary.

1. Definition (30 min)

A double skin facade adds a second skin, usually of glass, in front of (or behind) an ordinary facade. Between the two skins there is a ventilated cavity; its depth ranges from a few centimetres to 2 metres. If the cavity is not ventilated, it is just a very thick window.

Skin Typical
Outer skin Single glass, tempered or laminated; takes wind and rain
Cavity Blinds, airflow, maintenance walkway
Inner skin Curtain wall with insulating glass, opening vents

2. Why? (45 min)

Benefit Explanation
Solar control Blinds in the cavity are protected from wind and dirt; they catch heat in the cavity before it enters
Natural ventilation Windows can be opened even in tall buildings (the outer skin cuts the wind)
Acoustics Reduces outside noise in noisy places, even with windows open
Thermal buffer In winter the cavity is warmer than outside
Appearance A uniform, all-glass outer surface

3. Drawbacks (45 min)

Drawback Explanation
Cost Two skins, cavity equipment, more maintenance
Overheating A poorly ventilated cavity can become an oven in summer
Fire and smoke The cavity can become a channel for smoke between floors (day 5)
Cleaning Four glass surfaces to clean (day 6)
Lost floor area The facade depth takes lettable area

Rule: a double skin only makes sense if at least one of the benefits above is really needed in the project.

4. Types (45 min)

Type Cavity division Note
Box window Each window is a separate box; divided at floors and sides Best acoustic and fire separation
Shaft-box Box windows open into vertical shafts running several floors Ventilation by stack effect
Corridor Continuous horizontal corridor on each floor, divided between floors Mid-rise offices
Multi-storey Cavity continuous over many floors Strong stack effect; weak for fire and acoustics

In addition there is the closed cavity facade (CCF): the cavity is closed to the outside and supplied with dry, clean air; it is produced as unitized modules (day 6).

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 02

Ventilation

~3.5 h · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the cavity ventilation modes (natural, mechanical, hybrid), the airflow paths and the size of the stack effect. About 3 hours.

1. Ventilation modes (45 min)

Mode How the air moves
Natural Stack effect (warm air rises) and wind pressure
Mechanical Fans and the building ventilation system
Hybrid Natural first, fan-assisted when needed

2. Airflow paths (1 hour)

Path Air from → to When
Outdoor air curtain Enters from outside, passes the cavity, leaves outside Summer: remove heat from the cavity
Indoor air curtain Enters from inside, passes the cavity, returns inside or to the extract Winter: warm the inner skin surface
Supply From outside into the cavity, then inside Preheated fresh air
Exhaust From inside into the cavity, then outside Remove used air
Buffer Cavity closed Winter, night

The airflow path can be switched with dampers; an operating scenario is written for each season.

3. Stack effect (45 min)

The pressure difference produced by the stack effect is roughly:

ΔP ≈ ρ · g · h · (ΔT / T)

Example: h = 12 m (four floors), cavity 10 K warmer than outside, T ≈ 300 K, ρ = 1.2 kg/m³:

ΔP ≈ 1.2 × 9.81 × 12 × (10 / 300) ≈ 4.7 Pa

That is a very small pressure: wind pressures can be tens or hundreds of pascals. So the size of the openings and the effect of wind can only be assessed reliably with an airflow (CFD) or building energy simulation.

4. Openings (15 min)

  1. If inlet and outlet openings are stacked directly above each other, one floor's exhaust mixes into the next floor's inlet (short circuit). Stagger the openings or use deflectors.
  2. Openings must be protected against rain, snow and insects (louvres, grilles).

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 03

Sun

~3.5 h · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know how blinds in the cavity, glass selection and cavity temperature affect facade performance. About 3 hours.

1. Where the blinds go (1 hour)

Position Advantage Disadvantage
Outside Cuts heat best Wind, dirt, maintenance; difficult on tall buildings
In the cavity Protected; leaves heat in the cavity to be ventilated away Cavity heats up; ventilation is essential
Inside Cheap, easy The heat is already inside

The position of the blind in the cavity also matters: a blind close to the outer skin keeps the heated air away from the inner skin and makes it easier to exhaust.

2. Glass selection (45 min)

Skin Typical glass Why
Outer Single glass, tempered or laminated; can be low-iron Wind and impact; let solar radiation into the cavity
Inner Insulating glass, low-e Thermal insulation (U-value)

A solar control coating on the outer skin reduces cavity overheating but changes light and appearance. The total solar energy transmittance (g) of the system changes with the blind position; calculate it separately for blinds up and down.

3. Cavity temperature (45 min)

  1. In summer a poorly ventilated cavity can be much hotter than outside; the inner skin surface heats up and comfort suffers.
  2. In multi-storey types the upper floors are hotter (warm air rises).
  3. In winter the cavity works as a buffer; heat loss through the inner skin drops.

The design should check the cavity temperature for the hottest day (worst case) by simulation.

4. Daylight and view (15 min)

Two skins, blinds and coatings reduce light transmittance. Reflections and double images (between two glass surfaces) should be considered in the architectural approval.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 04

Structure and physics

~3.5 h · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know about wind load, acoustics, condensation, drainage and the supporting structure (brackets, walkways) of double skin facades. About 3 hours.

1. Wind load (45 min)

  1. The outer skin takes the wind load; it is designed for the full design load.
  2. The load on the inner skin depends on the cavity openings: with large openings the cavity pressure approaches the outside pressure and the inner skin also takes a significant load.
  3. Open and closed (dampers) conditions are assessed separately. For tall buildings wind tunnel data are used (Structural Basics, in Turkish).

2. Acoustics (45 min)

  1. The outer skin reduces noise through an open window; this is one of the strongest reasons for a double skin.
  2. Room to room sound: in corridor and multi-storey types sound can travel through the cavity to the next room and floor. The box window type is the best here.
  3. Ventilation openings are acoustic weak points; acoustic (attenuated) louvres can be used.

3. Condensation and water (45 min)

  1. In winter the outer skin is cold; if moist air leaks from inside into the cavity, it condenses on the inner face of the outer skin.
  2. Prevention: make the inner skin airtight; ventilate the cavity with outside air (which is cold and dry).
  3. Rain, snow and condensate entering the cavity need a sloped floor and drainage.

4. Structure and walkways (45 min)

  1. The outer skin is carried by brackets projecting from the slabs (steel or aluminium); brackets are thermal bridges, use thermal break pads.
  2. A maintenance walkway on each floor: grating floor you can walk on for cleaning and maintenance. Grating lets air through but must be assessed for fire and smoke (day 5).
  3. Walkway loads (people, equipment) and guardrails must be part of the design.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 05

Fire

~3.5 h · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the fire and smoke spread risk in double skin facades, how the types differ and what measures are taken. About 3 hours.

1. Risk (1 hour)

If a window breaks in a fire, hot gases and smoke enter the cavity. If the cavity is vertically continuous (multi-storey and shaft types), it works like a chimney and carries smoke and heat to the floors above. The outer skin also reflects heat back and heats the cavity further.

Type Vertical spread risk
Box window Low (each floor and window divided)
Corridor Low-medium (divided between floors; watch the opening layout)
Shaft-box High (shafts vertically continuous)
Multi-storey High

2. Measures (1 hour)

  1. Division between floors: fire-resistant horizontal barriers at each floor (or every few floors).
  2. Opening layout: one floor's outlet must not sit directly below the next floor's inlet.
  3. Dampers: dampers that close or open automatically in a fire (depending on the smoke strategy).
  4. Sprinklers: in some projects, in the cavity or in front of the inner skin.
  5. Materials: fire classes of blinds and other materials in the cavity.

3. Regulations and analysis (45 min)

  1. National fire regulations (in Turkey: the Regulation on Fire Protection of Buildings; see Fire Regulation, in Turkish) may not describe double skins in every detail; a fire strategy is prepared with a fire consultant.
  2. CFD fire and smoke simulation (e.g. with FDS) shows the effect of the design; research shows that cavity depth and opening geometry clearly affect smoke movement.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 06

Maintenance and CCF

~3.5 h · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the maintenance and cleaning needs of double skin facades, unitized double skins and the closed cavity facade (CCF). About 3 hours.

1. Cleaning (45 min)

A double skin has four glass surfaces to clean: both faces of the outer skin and both faces of the inner skin. For the two faces in the cavity:

  1. A maintenance walkway on each floor (day 4), or
  2. Inner vents opening into the cavity, or
  3. Openable panels in the outer skin.

Cleaning frequency and method must be decided in the design; without access the cavity faces are never cleaned (Facade Maintenance and Access, in Turkish).

2. Maintenance of cavity equipment (30 min)

Blind motors, dampers, sensors and cables are in the cavity: plan access, spare parts and how to replace a failed unit.

3. Unitized double skin (45 min)

A double skin can also be factory-made as unitized modules: inner and outer skins, blinds and cavity come in one module. Advantages: factory quality, fast installation, a thinner facade (less lost area). Module joints and the continuity of the cavity between modules must be solved in the design (Facade Installation, in Turkish).

4. Closed cavity facade (CCF) (45 min)

Feature CCF
Cavity Closed to the outside
Air A small continuous supply of dry, filtered air to the cavity (slight overpressure)
Purpose No condensation or dust; no cleaning inside the cavity
Blinds In the cavity, protected
Watch out Continuity and maintenance of the air supply; airtightness

Unlike a ventilated double skin, a CCF does not remove cavity heat with airflow; summer heating is controlled by the blinds, glass coatings and design. Thermal and condensation calculations must therefore be detailed.

End-of-day check (15 min)

Question If yes
Is the site very noisy and should windows open? Strong candidate
Is the building tall and is natural ventilation wanted? Candidate
Is external solar shading difficult (wind, maintenance)? Candidate
Are the budget and the lost area acceptable? Continue
