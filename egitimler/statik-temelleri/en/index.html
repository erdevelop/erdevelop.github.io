Structural Basics

A 7-day, diagram-rich structural course for reading and checking façade calculations. From force, moment and equilibrium to shear and moment diagrams, from section properties to stress and deflection, to bolt and bracket checks; all with worked examples on the same façade mullion. It prepares you for day 6 of the Façade Physics course and the site's Wind Load, Snow Load and Profile Section tools.

Façade designers, draughtspeople, bid and project engineers; anyone who wants to read and check structural calculation reports.

DAY 01

Force and load path

~4 hours · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know what a force is, units, how forces add up, the types of load on a façade and how load is transferred from glass to the building (load path). About 4 hours.

About this course: It explains basic structural concepts with façade examples. It is the background for day 6 of the Façade Physics course (Turkish) and for understanding the site's Wind Load, Snow Load and Profile Section tools. In real projects calculations are done by a qualified engineer to the relevant standards (EN 1990, EN 1991, EN 1999, EN 1993, TS 498); this course does not replace that, but lets you read and check the calculation.

1. Force and units (1 hour)

A force is an action that pushes or pulls a body. It has a magnitude, a line of action and a direction; that is why it is drawn as an arrow (vector).

Quantity Unit Façade example
Force N, kN (1 kN = 1000 N) Anchor reaction 3.24 kN
Distributed load (area) kN/m² = kPa Wind 1.2 kN/m²
Line load kN/m = N/mm Load on the mullion 1.8 kN/m
Moment kNm, Nmm 2.92 kNm in the mullion
Stress N/mm² = MPa 105 N/mm² in aluminium

Mass and weight: 1 kg of mass weighs about 9.81 N. In practice 100 kg ≈ 1 kN.

Unit traps: there is a factor of 10⁶ between kN/m² and N/mm². Writing units on every line of a calculation is the best antidote to errors. 1 kN/m is exactly 1 N/mm.

2. Adding forces (45 min)

  1. Forces on the same line are added algebraically.
  2. Perpendicular forces: The resultant is found with Pythagoras. The resultant of a 3 kN horizontal and a 4 kN vertical force is √(3² + 4²) = 5 kN.
  3. Resolving into components: An inclined force is split into horizontal and vertical components: Fx = F·cos α, Fy = F·sin α.

3. Loads on the façade (1 hour)

Load Type Source
Self-weight (G) Permanent Weight of glass, profiles, panels
Wind (W) Variable EN 1991-1-4; pressure and suction
Snow (S) Variable TS 498, EN 1991-1-3; canopy, sloped glass, eaves
Imposed load (Q) Variable Balustrade horizontal load, maintenance load
Temperature Variable Expansion, restraint
Earthquake (E) Accidental / special Turkish Building Earthquake Code

Example: 8 + 6 mm insulating glass weighs about 14 × 2.5 = 35 kg/m². A 1.5 × 2.5 m pane weighs 3.75 × 35 ≈ 131 kg ≈ 1.29 kN.

4. Load path (1 hour)

The first question in structures is: Where does the load enter and where does it leave?

  1. Wind pushes on the glass.
  2. The glass passes the load at its edges to the transoms and mullions.
  3. Transoms pass the load to the mullions.
  4. Mullions pass the load to the brackets and anchors.
  5. Anchors pass the load to the slab, and the slab to the building structure.

Each link gives load to the next and takes load from the previous one. The weakest link of the chain determines the façade's capacity. A detail with a gap in the load path (e.g. no setting block to carry the glass weight) does not work whatever the calculation says.

DAY 02

Moment and equilibrium

~4 hours · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know moment, the equilibrium equations, support types and how the support reactions of a beam are found. About 4 hours.

1. Moment (1 hour)

Moment = force × perpendicular distance to the line of the force. It is the turning effect of a force about a point.

Example: If the glass weight (1.29 kN) acts 100 mm outside the mullion face of the bracket, the moment in the mullion connection is:

M = F × e = 1.29 kN × 0.10 m ≈ 0.13 kNm

That is why eccentricity matters on façades: the closer you carry heavy glass to the mullion axis, the smaller the moment in the connection.

2. Equilibrium (1 hour)

A body at rest is in equilibrium. In the plane three equations are written:

ΣFx = 0     sum of horizontal forces is zero
ΣFy = 0     sum of vertical forces is zero
ΣM  = 0     sum of moments about any point is zero

Unknown support reactions are found with these three equations. Choosing a point through which an unknown force passes for taking moments removes that force from the equation and makes things easier.

3. Supports (1 hour)

Support Restrains Reactions On the façade
Roller (sliding) One direction 1 force Bottom (sliding) connection of the mullion, free vertically
Pinned (hinge) Two directions 2 forces Top (hanging) connection of the mullion
Fixed (built-in) Two directions + rotation 2 forces + 1 moment Cantilever canopy beam, steel cast into concrete

One end of the mullion is pinned and the other a roller so it can expand and contract with temperature (Façade Design Basics, day 4). Structurally this means a simple beam.

4. Support reactions (45 min)

Example: mullion. Span L = 3.6 m, wind on the mullion q = 1.8 kN/m (1.2 kN/m² × 1.5 m module).

Total load      Q = q × L = 1.8 × 3.6 = 6.48 kN
Symmetry        RA = RB = Q / 2 = 3.24 kN
Check (ΣM_A = 0):  RB × 3.6 = 6.48 × 1.8  →  RB = 3.24 kN  ✓

Each anchor takes 3.24 kN of horizontal load. This is the input for bracket and anchor selection.

Exercise: If an extra 1 kN point load (e.g. a sun shade) acts 1.2 m below the top support of the same mullion, what are the support reactions? (Answer: RA = 3.24 + 1 × 2.4/3.6 = 3.91 kN; RB = 3.24 + 1 × 1.2/3.6 = 3.57 kN.)

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 03

Internal forces

~4 hours · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the shear force and bending moment inside a beam, their diagrams and common beam formulas. About 4 hours.

1. Internal forces (1 hour)

Cut the beam in two with an imaginary section: for each part to stay in equilibrium there must be two internal forces at the section:

  1. Shear force (V): The force trying to slide the section in the perpendicular direction.
  2. Bending moment (M): The moment trying to rotate the section and bend the beam.

V and M are different at every point of the beam. Their graphs along the beam are called the shear force diagram and the bending moment diagram.

2. Simple beam, distributed load (1 hour)

Shear    V(x) = q × (L/2 − x)          largest at the ends:  V = q × L / 2
Moment   M(x) = q × x × (L − x) / 2    largest at mid-span:  M = q × L² / 8

Mullion example: q = 1.8 kN/m, L = 3.6 m

V_max = 1.8 × 3.6 / 2 = 3.24 kN       (at the supports)
M_max = 1.8 × 3.6² / 8 = 2.92 kNm     (at mid-span)

Rule: Where the shear force is zero, the moment is largest.

3. Common formulas (1.5 hours)

Case Max moment Max shear Max deflection
Simple beam, distributed q qL²/8 qL/2 5qL⁴ / (384EI)
Simple beam, point load P at mid-span PL/4 P/2 PL³ / (48EI)
Cantilever, distributed q qL²/2 qL qL⁴ / (8EI)
Cantilever, point load P at tip PL P PL³ / (3EI)
Simple beam, two equal P at distance a from the ends P·a P (day 5)

Example 1 — transom, glass weight: a 1.29 kN pane sits on two setting blocks, each a = 150 mm in from the transom end: P = 0.64 kN.

M = P × a = 0.64 × 0.15 ≈ 0.10 kNm     (about the transom's weak axis)

Example 2 — cantilever canopy beam: L = 1.5 m, snow s = 0.6 kN/m², beam spacing 1.2 m → q = 0.72 kN/m.

M = q × L² / 2 = 0.72 × 1.5² / 2 = 0.81 kNm    (at the fixed end)

4. Continuous beam, intuition (15 min)

A mullion continuous over two spans (continuous beam) has a smaller span moment and deflection than a simple beam of the same span; but a moment of opposite sign forms at the intermediate support. That is why the mullion splice is usually placed near the support, where the moment is small.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 04

Section properties

~4 hours · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will be able to calculate the area, centroid, moment of inertia and section modulus of a section and read the values in profile catalogues. About 4 hours.

1. Area and centroid (45 min)

  1. Area (A): The material area of the section. Weight = A × length × density (aluminium ≈ 2.7 g/cm³, steel ≈ 7.85 g/cm³).
  2. Centroid: The "balance point" of the section. In symmetric sections it lies on the axis of symmetry. In a composite section: ȳ = Σ(Ai × yi) / ΣAi.

2. Moment of inertia (1.5 hours)

The moment of inertia (I) shows the section's stiffness against bending. Its unit is mm⁴ or cm⁴ (1 cm⁴ = 10⁴ mm⁴).

Rectangle (b width, h depth):   I = b × h³ / 12

h cubed! Doubling the depth makes the stiffness eight times larger. That is why mullions are deep in the wind direction.

Box section: Subtract the inner void from the outer rectangle.

Example: 50 × 150 × 3 mm box mullion (h = 150 mm in the wind direction)

I = (50 × 150³ − 44 × 144³) / 12
  = (168 750 000 − 131 383 296) / 12 ≈ 3 113 900 mm⁴ ≈ 311 cm⁴
A = 50 × 150 − 44 × 144 = 1 164 mm²   →   weight ≈ 1 164 × 2.7 / 1000 ≈ 3.1 kg/m

3. Steiner's theorem (45 min)

To find the moment of inertia of a part about an axis that does not pass through its own centroid:

I = I_own + A × d²

Example: a 100 × 10 mm flange, d = 50 mm from the axis:

I_own = 100 × 10³ / 12 ≈ 8 333 mm⁴
A × d²  = 1 000 × 50² = 2 500 000 mm⁴
I       ≈ 2 508 000 mm⁴ ≈ 251 cm⁴

Almost all of the contribution comes from A × d²: putting material far from the axis is the most efficient way. This is the logic of I-sections and deep box mullions.

4. Section modulus (45 min)

The section modulus (W) is used to calculate the section's bending strength:

W = I / c          c: distance of the extreme fibre from the neutral axis

For the 50 × 150 × 3 mullion c = 75 mm:

W = 3 113 900 / 75 ≈ 41 500 mm³ ≈ 41.5 cm³

Reading catalogues: Profile catalogues give Ix, Iy, Wx, Wy. Check which axis is in the wind direction. In a mullion, wind usually bends about the strong axis. You can calculate values for standard sections with the site's Profile Section tool.

DAY 05

Stress and safety

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will be able to calculate bending and shear stress, know material strength, partial safety factors and load combinations, and check the strength of a mullion. About 4 hours.

1. Bending stress (1 hour)

In a bending beam one side is compressed and the other stretched; in between is the neutral axis where stress is zero. Stress increases with distance from the neutral axis and is largest at the extreme fibres:

σ = M / W

2. Shear stress (30 min)

Shear force is carried mainly by the webs of the section (the walls in the wind direction). Roughly:

τ ≈ V / A_web

Shear stress is usually small in façade mullions. It can matter in short, heavily loaded elements (brackets, cantilevers).

3. Material strength (45 min)

Material Modulus of elasticity E Yield (example)
Aluminium EN AW-6063 T6 70 000 N/mm² f0.2 ≈ 160 N/mm²
Aluminium EN AW-6060 T66 70 000 N/mm² f0.2 ≈ 150 N/mm²
Structural steel S235 210 000 N/mm² fy = 235 N/mm²
Structural steel S355 210 000 N/mm² fy = 355 N/mm²

Aluminium values vary with section thickness and heat treatment; in projects the tables in EN 1999-1-1 are used. The strength of aluminium drops considerably in welded zones.

4. Safety: load and material factors (1 hour)

In the Eurocode approach safety is provided from two sides:

  1. Loads are increased: Design load = characteristic load × γF. Typical values: 1.35 for permanent load, 1.5 for variable load.
  2. Strength is reduced: Design strength = characteristic strength / γM. Typical γM1 = 1.1 for aluminium; 1.0 for steel.

Load combination (ultimate limit state, basic form):

1.35 × G  +  1.5 × Q_leading  +  1.5 × ψ0 × Q_other

For example, if wind and snow act together one is leading and the other is reduced by the ψ0 factor. Factors are taken from EN 1990 and its national annex. Deflection is checked with unfactored (characteristic) load (day 6).

5. Mullion strength check (45 min)

Characteristic moment  M_k  = 2.92 kNm                 (day 3)
Design moment          M_Ed = 1.5 × 2.92 = 4.37 kNm
Section                W    = 41.5 cm³                  (day 4, 50 × 150 × 3)
Stress                 σ    = 4.37 × 10⁶ / 41 500 ≈ 105 N/mm²
Design strength        f_d  = 160 / 1.1 ≈ 145 N/mm²
Utilisation            105 / 145 ≈ 0.73   →  strength SUFFICIENT

Shear check: V_Ed = 1.5 × 3.24 = 4.86 kN, two webs 2 × 3 × 144 = 864 mm² → τ ≈ 5.6 N/mm², very small.

The mullion is sufficient in strength. But it is not finished yet: deflection (day 6).

DAY 06

Deflection

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know what deflection is, the deflection limits on façades, the difference between stiffness and strength and why deflection usually governs on façades. About 4 hours.

1. Deflection (1 hour)

Deflection (δ) is the displacement of a beam as it bends under load. Simple beam, distributed load:

δ = 5 × q × L⁴ / (384 × E × I)
  • L⁴: Increasing the span by 10% increases deflection by about 46%.
  • E: The stiffness of the material. Steel's E is about 3 times that of aluminium.
  • I: The stiffness of the section (day 4).

2. Deflection limits on façades (45 min)

Common limit for curtain wall framing members (EN 13830 and EN 13116):

Span L Limit
L ≤ 3 m L / 200
3 m < L < 7.5 m 5 mm + L / 300
L ≥ 7.5 m L / 250

There may also be limits required by the glass manufacturer at the glass edge support. Deflection is checked with characteristic load (unfactored).

3. Mullion deflection check (1 hour)

q = 1.8 N/mm, L = 3600 mm, E = 70 000 N/mm², I = 3 113 900 mm⁴  (50 × 150 × 3)

δ = 5 × 1.8 × 3600⁴ / (384 × 70 000 × 3 113 900) ≈ 18.1 mm
Limit = 5 + 3600 / 300 = 17 mm
18.1 > 17   →  deflection NOT SUFFICIENT

The same mullion was comfortable in strength with 73% utilisation (day 5), but it fails in deflection. Required moment of inertia:

I_required = 5 × 1.8 × 3600⁴ / (384 × 70 000 × 17) ≈ 331 cm⁴

4. Options (45 min)

Option Result
Deeper profile: 60 × 180 × 3 (I ≈ 545 cm⁴) δ ≈ 10.3 mm ✓
Steel reinforcement in the same profile Stiffness increases; weight and thermal mismatch need attention
Steel profile of the same size (E three times) δ ≈ 6.0 mm ✓, but heavy and needs corrosion protection
Intermediate anchor (shortening the span) L⁴ effect: the strongest option, but an extra connection on the building side

Lesson: Because E is low in aluminium, deflection usually governs façade mullions. First find the required I from deflection, then check strength.

5. Snow load and sloped glass (15 min)

On canopies and sloped glass snow loads in addition to wind: s = μ × sk (shape coefficient × ground snow load). On a canopy next to a high wall snow can drift and the load can increase several times. The site's Snow Load tool does this calculation to TS 498 and EN 1991-1-3. Use the load you find with the cantilever formulas of day 3.

DAY 07

Connections and the whole calculation

~4 hours · 0/4 checks

Goal

On the last day you learn the basic checks of connections (bolts, brackets) and do a full structural check of a mullion. Then you connect to day 6 of Façade Physics and the site's calculation tools. About 4 hours.

1. Bolts (1 hour)

A bolt on a façade is stressed in three ways:

  1. Shear: The connected plates try to cut the bolt shank like scissors.
  2. Bearing: The bolt crushes the thin material at the hole edge. In thin aluminium walls this usually governs.
  3. Tension: Suction load tries to open the connection.

Edge distances: There must be enough material between the hole and the edge (typically e1 ≥ 1.2 d0 or more; values from the standard). A hole too close to the edge tears the plate.

2. Bracket (1 hour)

The 3.24 kN horizontal load at the mullion connection creates a moment in the bracket arm:

Design load     P_Ed = 1.5 × 3.24 = 4.86 kN
Lever arm       e = 80 mm (distance of the load from the bracket root)
Moment          M_Ed = 4.86 × 0.08 ≈ 0.39 kNm
Plate 150 × 10  W = 150 × 10² / 6 = 2 500 mm³
Stress          σ = 0.39 × 10⁶ / 2 500 ≈ 156 N/mm²
S235 steel      f_d = 235 / 1.0 = 235 N/mm²   →  utilisation ≈ 0.66

The bracket is also checked for the capacity of the anchor in concrete (anchor manufacturer's software or approved values) and for the welds.

3. Mullion: start to finish (1.5 hours)

1. Load        w = 1.2 kN/m² (wind, characteristic; from the Wind Load tool)
2. Load path   q = w × b = 1.2 × 1.5 = 1.8 kN/m
3. Statics     simple beam, L = 3.6 m
4. Forces      M = qL²/8 = 2.92 kNm ; V = qL/2 = 3.24 kN
5. Deflection  I_required ≥ 331 cm⁴ → choose 60 × 180 × 3 (I ≈ 545 cm⁴), δ ≈ 10.3 mm ✓
6. Strength    W = 545/9 ≈ 60.6 cm³ → σ = 4.37 × 10⁶ / 60 600 ≈ 72 N/mm² ≤ 145 ✓
7. Support     each anchor 3.24 kN (characteristic), 4.86 kN (design) → bracket and anchor
8. Other       glass weight (transom), thermal movement, earthquake drift

This flow is the basis of day 6 of the Façade Physics course (Turkish). There you will see the same mullion together with how the wind load is found and the structural silicone calculation.

5. Final word (15 min)

In the end structures come down to four questions: What is the load? Where does it go? Will the element bend too much (deflection)? Will it break (strength)? This course taught you to ask these questions and to check the calculation. In projects the final word belongs to the qualified engineer calculating to the relevant standards.
