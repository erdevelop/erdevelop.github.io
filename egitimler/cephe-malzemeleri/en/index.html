Façade Materials

A diagram-rich 7-day programme explaining why the metals and auxiliary materials used in façades are chosen. You learn aluminium alloys and tempers, structural steel and galvanizing, stainless steel grades and fasteners, corrosion types and surface finishes, non-metallic materials such as polyamide and composite panels, and certificates, and finally make a justified material selection for your own project.

Before you start

Who is it for

Façade designers, project and purchasing engineers, quality control teams. No prior knowledge needed; Structural Basics helps.

What you need

  • A calculator.
  • Location and specification data of your own project for the final exercise.

DAY 01

Material properties

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the basic properties used to compare materials: modulus of elasticity, yield and tensile strength, elongation, density, thermal expansion and thermal conductivity. You will be able to read a stress-strain curve. Time about 4 hours.

About this course: It explains why the metals and auxiliary materials used in façades are chosen: aluminium alloys and tempers, structural steel and galvanizing, stainless steel and fasteners, corrosion, surface finishes, polymers and fire classes. It complements the Aluminium Profile Design, Sheet Metal and Bending and Structural Basics courses (in Turkish). Values in the tables are typical; for design use the values of the relevant standard and the manufacturer's certificate.

1. Stress-strain curve (1.5 hours)

When a bar is pulled:

  1. Elastic range: Stress is proportional to strain (σ = E × ε). When the load is removed the bar returns to its original length. The slope E is the modulus of elasticity.
  2. Yield: Permanent deformation begins. Structural steel has a distinct yield strength (fy); aluminium does not, so Rp0.2, the stress at 0.2% permanent strain, is used.
  3. Strain hardening and fracture: The highest stress is the tensile strength (Rm or fu); then necking and fracture.
  4. Elongation at fracture (A): Shows how ductile the material is; a ductile material warns before failing (it deforms a lot).

Façade calculations usually stay in the elastic range; the yield strength is used as the limit (Structural Basics, day 5).

2. Property table (1.5 hours)

Material E (N/mm²) Density (kg/m³) α (10⁻⁶/K) λ (W/mK)
Aluminium (6060) 70 000 2700 23 ~200
Structural steel (S235) 210 000 7850 12 ~50
Stainless (1.4401) 200 000 8000 16 ~15
Glass (soda-lime) 70 000 2500 9 1,0

3. What does it mean? (45 min)

  1. Stiffness: Aluminium's E is one third of steel's. With the same section and the same load an aluminium mullion deflects three times as much.
  2. Specific stiffness: E / density is ≈ 25.9 for aluminium and ≈ 26.8 for steel: almost the same. An aluminium section giving the same deflection weighs about the same if the section depth stays the same; aluminium's real advantages are complex extruded sections and corrosion resistance.
  3. Thermal expansion: A 50 K temperature change on a 3 m member: aluminium grows 3.45 mm, stainless 2.4 mm, steel 1.8 mm. If different materials are rigidly fixed together, this difference becomes stress (Joints and Sealing).
  4. Thermal conductivity: Aluminium conducts heat about 4 times better than steel and about 13 times better than stainless: this is why aluminium profiles need a thermal break (polyamide) (day 6).

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 02

Aluminium alloys

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the aluminium alloy series, the alloys used in façades, temper (heat treatment) designations and the effect of welding on strength. Time about 4 hours.

1. Alloy series (1 hour)

Wrought aluminium alloys are designated with a four-digit number (EN 573); the first digit is the main alloying element:

Series Main element Heat treatable In façades
1xxx Pure aluminium (≥ 99%) No Foil, some coatings
3xxx Manganese No (cold working) Sheet, coil-coated sheet (3003, 3105)
5xxx Magnesium No Sheet and plate (5005 anodizing quality, 5754)
6xxx Magnesium + silicon Yes Extruded profiles (6060, 6063, 6005A, 6082)
7xxx Zinc Yes Very high strength; rare in façades

Heat-treatable alloys (6xxx, 7xxx) gain strength by ageing; non-heat-treatable ones (3xxx, 5xxx) by cold working (work hardening).

2. Temper designations (1 hour)

Designation Meaning
F As fabricated
O Annealed, softest
H14, H24 Strain hardened (H1x) / strain hardened and partially annealed (H2x); the second digit is hardness (4 = half hard)
T4 Solution treated, naturally aged
T5 Cooled from extrusion temperature, artificially aged
T6 Solution treated, artificially aged
T66 Higher-strength variant of T6 (for 6060 and 6063)

For sheet, H tempers determine bendability (soft tempers bend easily); for profiles, T tempers determine strength.

3. Profile alloys (1.5 hours)

Some minimum values from EN 755-2 (vary with section thickness; approximate, N/mm²):

Alloy-temper Rp0.2 Rm Use
6060 T6 ~140-150 ~170-190 Windows, doors, thin-walled profiles; very good surface
6060 T66 ~150-160 ~195-215 Curtain wall profiles
6063 T6 ~170 ~215 Higher strength, good surface
6005A T6 ~200-215 ~250-255 Structural profiles
6082 T6 ~250-260 ~290-310 Brackets, highly loaded parts; lower surface quality
  1. As strength increases, extrusion becomes harder and anodized surface quality drops; 6060/6063 are preferred for visible profiles.
  2. In structural design characteristic values are taken from Eurocode 9 (EN 1999-1-1); design values are divided by the material factor.

4. Effect of welding and heat (15 min)

In 6xxx alloys welding destroys the ageing effect around the weld (heat-affected zone, HAZ); strength can drop to about half. This is why bolts and rivets are preferred in structural aluminium connections; if there is welding, a HAZ reduction is applied in the calculation.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 03

Steel and galvanizing

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know structural steel grades, hot-dip galvanizing, corrosivity categories and how to roughly estimate the life of galvanizing. Time about 4 hours.

1. Structural steel (1 hour)

Grade (EN 10025-2) fy (N/mm², t ≤ 40 mm) fu (N/mm²) In façades
S235 235 360 Brackets, anchor plates, substructure
S275 275 430
S355 355 490 Highly loaded brackets, structural steel
  1. The number in the name (235) is the yield strength (for thin sections).
  2. The extra letters (JR, J0, J2) show the impact toughness and the test temperature; important in cold climates and for thick welded parts.
  3. Steel does not resist corrosion; it is not used unprotected outdoors or in the façade cavity.

2. Hot-dip galvanizing (1 hour)

The part is dipped into molten zinc; zinc-iron alloy layers form on the steel surface with pure zinc on the outside (EN ISO 1461).

Steel thickness Minimum average coating
> 6 mm 85 µm
3-6 mm 70 µm
1.5-3 mm 55 µm
< 1.5 mm 45 µm
  1. Zinc protects steel both as a barrier and cathodically: even small scratches are protected by the surrounding zinc.
  2. Cutting, drilling and welding after galvanizing destroy the protection; holes and welds are repaired on site with zinc-rich paint. Better: do all fabrication before galvanizing.
  3. Electro-galvanizing (thin, ~5-15 µm) and zinc flake coatings are thinner; hot-dip is preferred for structural parts outdoors.

3. Corrosivity categories (1 hour)

EN ISO 9223 / 12944 classify environments by corrosivity. Approximate annual zinc loss:

Category Environment Zinc loss (µm/year)
C2 Low: rural, dry 0,1-0,7
C3 Medium: urban, light industry, away from the coast 0,7-2,1
C4 High: industrial, coastal 2,1-4,2
C5 Very high: heavy industry, seaside 4,2-8,4

4. Life estimate (45 min)

85 µm galvaniz, C4 ortam:
  en kötü:  85 / 4,2 ≈ 20 yıl
  en iyi:   85 / 2,1 ≈ 40 yıl
Aynı kaplama C3'te (0,7-2,1):  yaklaşık 40-120 yıl

A bracket in the façade cavity is not wetted by rain directly but is affected by humidity, condensation and salty air; the environment category is set at the start of the project. For longer life choose galvanizing + paint (a duplex system) or stainless steel (day 4).

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 04

Stainless steel

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know stainless steel types and grades, pitting resistance (PREN), selection in coastal environments and the meaning of stainless fastener markings (A2, A4, 70, 80). Time about 4 hours.

1. Why is stainless stainless? (45 min)

Stainless steel contains at least about 10.5% chromium. Chromium forms a very thin, self-healing passive oxide layer on the surface. When this layer breaks down (chloride, dirt, iron dust contamination, narrow gaps without oxygen) stainless steel rusts too.

2. Types and grades (1 hour)

Grade (EN 10088) AISI Type In façades
1.4301 304 Austenitic, ~18% Cr, ~8% Ni Interiors, rural and urban outdoors
1.4401 / 1.4404 316 / 316L Austenitic + ~2% Mo Coastal, industrial, around swimming pools
1.4462 2205 Duplex (~22% Cr, ~3% Mo) Very aggressive environments, high strength
1.4016 430 Ferritic, nickel-free Low-cost interior applications

Austenitic stainless steels are not magnetic (they may become slightly magnetic after cold working); a "magnet test" does not prove the grade, a certificate is needed (day 7).

3. PREN (1 hour)

Resistance to pitting corrosion is roughly compared with PREN:

PREN = %Cr + 3,3 × %Mo + 16 × %N

1.4301 (304):  18 + 0 + ~0,8          ≈ 18-19
1.4401 (316):  17 + 3,3 × 2,1         ≈ 24
1.4462 (2205): 22 + 3,3 × 3,1 + 16 × 0,17 ≈ 35

Molybdenum (Mo) has a large effect: moving from 304 to 316 makes a clear difference in coastal environments. Rule: outdoors within a few kilometres of the sea or where de-icing salt is used, consider at least grade 316. Selection considers the environment and maintenance (rain washing) together (as in the corrosion resistance factor method of EN 1993-1-4).

4. Fasteners (1 hour)

Stainless bolt marking according to EN ISO 3506-1: A4-70.

Part Meaning
A2 Austenitic, 304-type material
A4 Austenitic with Mo, 316-type material
-70 Tensile strength 700 N/mm² (Rp0.2 ≥ 450)
-80 Tensile strength 800 N/mm² (Rp0.2 ≥ 600)
  1. Outdoors usually A4-70 or A4-80.
  2. Galling: Stainless bolts and nuts can seize and cold-weld while being tightened. Prevention: lubrication or coated nuts, low tightening speed.
  3. Stainless bolts combined with aluminium or galvanized steel are assessed for galvanic corrosion (day 5).

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 05

Corrosion and finishes

~4 hours · 0/5 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the types of corrosion in façades (galvanic, crevice, filiform), ways to prevent galvanic corrosion and aluminium surface finishes (anodizing, powder coating, PVDF). Time about 4 hours.

1. Galvanic corrosion (1.5 hours)

When two different metals touch in an electrolyte (rainwater, condensation, salt water) a small battery forms; the less noble (anodic) metal corrodes.

More noble (cathodic) ← → Less noble (anodic)
Stainless steel (passive), copper Lead, tin Structural steel Aluminium, zinc

Three things determine the risk:

  1. Potential difference: How far apart are they in the series?
  2. Area ratio: Small anode + large cathode is bad (aluminium rivet in stainless sheet). Large anode + small cathode is usually acceptable (stainless screw in aluminium profile).
  3. Time of wetness: Risk is low in a joint that stays dry and high in a joint that holds water and does not drain.

Measures: Insulating separators (EPDM, PA or PTFE washers and bushes), drainage, painting the cathode (not the anode), compatible fasteners.

2. Other corrosion types (45 min)

Type Where Measure
Crevice Narrow, oxygen-free gaps: under washers, overlapping sheets Close or open the gaps, drainage
Pitting Stainless steel and aluminium in chloride environments Right grade (day 4), rain washing
Filiform Thread-like corrosion on painted aluminium, creeping under the paint from edges and cuts Correct pretreatment (chrome-free conversion coating, pre-anodizing), seaside-class powder coating

3. Anodizing (45 min)

  1. A thick oxide layer is grown electrochemically on the aluminium surface; the layer is part of the metal and does not peel.
  2. Thickness class (µm): 15 (interior), 20 (exterior), 25 (aggressive, coastal). Quality label: QUALANOD.
  3. Colour: natural, electrolytic colouring (bronze to black shades) and dyeing.
  4. Alloy and batch differences can cause colour shade differences; for visible façades require material from the same batch and an approved colour range (samples).

4. Powder coating and PVDF (45 min)

Polyester powder coating PVDF (liquid or coil)
Thickness Typically at least 60 µm 2-3 coats, ~35-40 µm
Colour and gloss retention By class (QUALICOAT Class 1, 2, 3) Very good
Coastal environment "Seaside" pretreatment class Common

In powder coating quality, pretreatment (surface preparation) is at least as important as the paint; most filiform corrosion comes from pretreatment.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 06

Non-metallic materials

~4 hours · 0/4 checks

Goal

By the end of the day you will know the non-metallic materials in façades: thermal breaks (polyamide), gaskets and sealants, composite panel cores, insulation materials and reaction-to-fire classes. Time about 4 hours.

1. Thermal break (1 hour)

Because aluminium conducts heat very well (day 1), the inner and outer profiles are separated by a low-conductivity part:

  1. Polyamide PA66 GF25: polyamide reinforced with 25% glass fibre; λ ≈ 0.3 W/mK, thermal expansion close to aluminium. It is joined to the profiles by crimping.
  2. The thermal break has a shear strength; the bending stiffness of the composite profile depends on the shear transferred through the break. Use the values given by the system supplier.
  3. The thermal break must withstand paint and anodizing oven temperatures; the sequence (coating first or assembly first?) is agreed with the supplier.

2. Gaskets and sealants (45 min)

Details in the Joints and Sealing course. From the material point of view:

Material Property
EPDM UV and ozone resistant; not oil resistant
Silicone Very wide temperature range; structural silicone
TPE Recyclable, weldable
Butyl Very low vapour permeability

3. Composite panel core (1 hour)

Aluminium composite panel: a core between two thin aluminium sheets. The core determines fire behaviour:

Core Typical fire class
Polyethylene (PE) Combustible; must not be used on tall buildings
Mineral-filled (FR) Around B-s1,d0
Mainly mineral (A2) A2-s1,d0

The product's own declared class and certificate govern. Regulatory requirements for external cladding on tall buildings are covered in the Fire and Regulations course.

4. Insulation and fire classes (1 hour)

EN 13501-1 reaction-to-fire classes:

Class Meaning Example
A1 Non-combustible Stone wool (most), glass, metal
A2 Limited combustibility A2 composite panel
B, C, D, E, F Increasing combustibility Polymers, foam insulation
s1, s2, s3 Smoke production (s1 least)
d0, d1, d2 Burning droplets (d0 none)

Stone wool (A1) in the façade cavity and in slab-edge fire barriers; foams such as EPS and XPS only in limited places defined by regulations.

End-of-day check (15 min)

DAY 07

Selection and certificates

~4 hours · 0/4 checks

Goal

On the last day you bring material selection together: selection matrix, material certificates (EN 10204), specification wording, common mistakes and a final exercise. Time about 4 hours.

1. Selection matrix (1 hour)

Element Environment C2-C3 Environment C4-C5 (coastal, industrial)
Visible profile 6060/6063 T66, anodized 20 µm or powder coated 6060/6063, anodized 25 µm or seaside-class powder coating / PVDF
Bracket Galvanized S235 or 6082 T6 aluminium Galvanized + painted (duplex), 316 stainless or 6082
Fastener A2-70 A4-70 / A4-80
Hidden substructure Galvanized steel or aluminium Aluminium or duplex
Sheet cladding 5005 anodized, 3105 coil coated 5005 / 5754, seaside pretreatment

This table is a starting point; the project specification, the system supplier and the structural calculation govern.

2. Certificates (45 min)

EN 10204 inspection documents:

Type Content Who validates
2.1 Declaration of compliance, no test results Manufacturer
2.2 Non-specific test results Manufacturer
3.1 Specific test results for that batch Manufacturer's independent quality department
3.2 3.1 + independent inspector's approval Manufacturer + independent inspector

For structural steel, aluminium and stainless parts 3.1 is usually required. The heat (cast) number on the certificate must match the number on the material.

3. Common mistakes (1 hour)

  1. Aluminium rivets in stainless sheet (small anode, large cathode).
  2. Bracket ends and holes cut after galvanizing and left unpainted.
  3. Grade 304 stainless or A2 bolts on the coast.
  4. Joints that hold water and do not drain (crevice corrosion).
  5. Anodizing shade differences between batches on visible profiles.
  6. PE-core composite panels on a tall building.
  7. Long aluminium and steel members rigidly fixed together (difference in thermal expansion).
  8. Material without a certificate or with non-matching batch numbers.

4. Final exercise (45 min + your own time)

  1. Determine the corrosivity category of your own project (location, distance from the sea, industry).
  2. Choose material and finish for the visible profile, bracket, fastener and cladding; write the justification.
  3. If the bracket is galvanized, calculate its life with the day 3 method.
  4. List every joint where different metals touch and write the galvanic measure.
  5. A specification paragraph: alloy-temper, finish class, fastener grade, certificate type.

End-of-day check (15 min)
