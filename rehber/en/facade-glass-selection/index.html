How to choose façade glass

A glass data sheet lists dozens of values. Here are the ones that really matter in façade design, and when each glass type is used.

  • Keep Ug low (winter heat loss) and choose g by orientation and climate (summer heat gain).
  • Aim for a high Tv for daylight and a selectivity Tv/g ≥ 1.8 for efficiency.
  • For sound: asymmetric thicknesses, a wider cavity and laminated glass with acoustic PVB.
  • Laminated glass where safety is needed, heat-strengthened glass where there is a thermal breakage risk.

1. Heat: Ug

The heat passing through one square metre of glass for a 1 K temperature difference (W/m²K, EN 673). A low-E, argon-filled double glazed unit gives about 1.0–1.1; triple glazing 0.5–0.7. The value of the whole window, Uw, is calculated with the frame (Uf) and the glass edge thermal bridge (ψ).

2. Sun: g and Tv

g (solar factor) is the share of incoming solar energy that passes inside. On south and west façades of highly glazed offices 0.25–0.40 is typical; in housing, higher values may be preferred to benefit from winter sun. Tv is the share of daylight transmitted; below 0.50 the interior can feel dark. The ratio of the two is selectivity: less heat for the same daylight.

3. Sound: Rw

The weighted sound reduction index (dB) is given according to EN ISO 717-1. More mass and two panes of different thickness reduce resonance; laminated glass with an acoustic PVB interlayer makes a 3–5 dB difference. For traffic noise, Rw+Ctr is the more relevant value.

4. Glass types

TypeWhen?
Float (annealed)Unprocessed glass; breaks into large sharp pieces. Inside laminates or in low-risk locations.
Heat strengthenedAbout twice the strength of float; large pieces remain when broken. For dark or partly shaded glass with thermal breakage risk, inside laminates.
Toughened (tempered)4–5 times the strength of float; breaks into small fragments. Doors, low-level zones, thermal stress. A heat soak test is recommended against nickel sulphide risk.
LaminatedPVB/SGP between two panes; fragments stay on the interlayer when broken. Balustrades, sloped glazing, overhead glass, safety and sound.

5. Thickness and weight

Thickness is calculated from wind load, pane size and support type (EN 16612, DIN 18008). Each millimetre of glass weighs 2.5 kg/m²; an 8/16/44.2 double glazed unit weighs about 42 kg/m², which governs setting blocks and installation equipment.
