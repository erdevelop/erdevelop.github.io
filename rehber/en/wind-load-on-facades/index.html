How is wind load on a façade calculated?

Wind load is the starting point for curtain wall, glass and bracket design. This guide walks through the part of EN 1991-1-4 a façade designer needs, in a simple order.

  • From the basic wind velocity (vb), the peak velocity pressure qp(z) is found for the height and terrain.
  • Surface pressure is we = qp · cpe; suction is highest at the corners (zone A).
  • For the net load, internal pressure (cpi) is also taken into account.
  • For glass and anchor strength checks the load is multiplied by the 1.5 safety factor.

1. Basic wind velocity

The basic wind velocity vb,0 is the 10-minute mean velocity at 10 m above open terrain with a 50-year return period. It is given by the national annex or wind map of each country; in Turkey 28 m/s is used for many regions in practice, with higher values on coasts and in windy areas. Direction and season factors are usually 1: vb = vb,0.

qb = ½ · ρ · vb² (ρ = 1.25 kg/m³) → for 28 m/s, qb = 0.49 kN/m²

2. Terrain category and height

Wind slows down and becomes more turbulent over rough terrain. The standard defines five categories: 0 (sea), I (lakes, flat open land), II (low vegetation, isolated buildings), III (villages, suburbs, forest), IV (urban areas with at least 15% of the surface covered by buildings taller than 15 m). Mean velocity increases with height z; the top of the façade is used as reference (on tall, wide buildings the façade can be divided into zones by height).

qp(z) = [1 + 7·Iv(z)] · ½ · ρ · vm(z)²

For vb = 28 m/s and 30 m height, qp is about 1.2 kN/m² in category III and about 1.5 kN/m² in category II.

3. External pressure coefficients (cpe)

On vertical walls the windward face (D) is under pressure, while the side faces (A, B, C) and the leeward face (E) are under suction. Zone A is e/5 wide from the building corner (e = the smaller of b and 2h) and has the highest suction. Typical values for loaded areas above 10 m² (h/d ≈ 1):

For small loaded areas such as glass panes and fixings, values between 1 and 10 m² are interpolated logarithmically.

4. Internal pressure and net load

For closed buildings the more unfavourable of +0.2 and −0.3 is usually taken as the internal pressure coefficient cpi. The net load is:

wnet = qp(z) · (cpe − cpi)

Example: qp = 1.0 kN/m², zone A cpe = −1.2, cpi = +0.2 → wnet = −1.4 kN/m² (suction). For the ultimate limit state, γQ = 1.5 gives a design load of 2.1 kN/m²; deflection checks use the characteristic load.

5. Where is it used on a façade?

  • Glass thickness and deflection (with the short edge and support type).
  • Bending and deflection checks of mullions and transoms.
  • Bracket, anchor and dowel loads (suction as tension, pressure as compression).
  • Structural silicone bite width.
