Curtain wall systems: which one when?

The first decision in a façade project is the system. This guide compares the common systems, how they work and which projects they suit.

Erdal Üstünol · 18.11.2026Türkçe
🌬️ Wind Load🧴 Joint & Sealant🧩 Façade Division🧪 Performance Classes
  • Stick (pressure plate): mullions and transoms assembled on site; economical, low to mid-rise buildings.
  • Structural silicone: no profile visible outside; the glass is held by silicone.
  • Unitised: factory-made modules, fast installation, tall buildings.
  • Ventilated façade: a ventilated cavity between cladding and wall; opaque surfaces.
  • Double skin: an air corridor between two façades; acoustic and energy performance.

1. Stick system

Mullions hang from the floor slabs on brackets, transoms are fixed to the mullions, and the glass is clamped from inside or outside with a pressure plate and cover cap. The thermal break sits between the pressure plate and the load-bearing profile. The advantages are flexibility and cost; the drawbacks are intensive site labour and weather dependency. Movement joints are left at mullion splices (usually 15–20 mm).

2. Structural silicone glazing

The glass is bonded to a frame profile with structural silicone; from outside only glass and joints are visible. The silicone bite is calculated from wind suction and pane size (ETAG 002 / EN 15434), and the dead load is usually carried by setting blocks. Tall buildings may require a mechanical safety retainer.

3. Unitised façade

Glass, profiles and gaskets are assembled into storey-high modules in the factory; on site they are lifted by crane and interlocked. Quality control is easier and installation is fast and scaffold-free, but design, testing (mock-up) and logistics take longer. Module joints are designed to take floor movements and thermal expansion.

4. Ventilated façade (rainscreen)

Composite panels, ceramic, stone, HPL or metal cladding are hung on a sub-structure, with thermal insulation and a ventilated cavity behind. The cavity handles condensation and equalises rain pressure. For fire safety, the reaction-to-fire class of cladding and insulation and the cavity fire barriers are critical.

5. Double-skin façade

There is an air corridor of 20 cm to 1 m between the inner and outer façade, which can house sun shading. It is effective for sound insulation and natural ventilation; cost, cleaning and summer overheating need attention.

6. Order in the project process

  1. Architectural and performance specification (wind, U value, acoustics, fire, watertightness classes).
  2. System selection and principle details.
  3. Structural and thermal calculations.
  4. Mock-up and performance tests.
  5. Shop drawings, orders and quantity take-off.
  6. Installation, site inspections and as-built drawings.

Each stage is covered in detail in the site's façade courses (Turkish).
