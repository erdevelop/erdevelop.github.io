Thermal expansion and joint width

Façade elements lengthen in summer and shorten in winter. If the joint is not sized for this movement, the sealant tears or the panel gets jammed.

Erdal Üstünol · 18.11.2026Türkçe
🌡️ Thermal Expansion🧴 Joint & Sealant
  • ΔL = α · L · ΔT; aluminium expands about twice as much as steel.
  • Joint width = total movement divided by the sealant's movement capability.
  • Depth is usually half the width (2:1), with a backer rod behind.

Expansion coefficients

Materialα (1/K)ΔL for 3 m, 50 K
Aluminium23–24 × 10⁻⁶≈ 3.5 mm
Steel12 × 10⁻⁶≈ 1.8 mm
Stainless (austenitic)16 × 10⁻⁶≈ 2.4 mm
Glass9 × 10⁻⁶≈ 1.4 mm
Concrete10–12 × 10⁻⁶≈ 1.7 mm

Temperature range

The calculation uses the difference between the installation temperature and the extreme temperatures. Dark, sun-exposed metal surfaces can reach 70–80 °C in summer and drop below −10 °C in winter, so design ranges of ±40 K and more are common on façades.

Joint width

Joint width ≥ Total movement ÷ Movement capability

With a ±25% sealant, 3.5 mm of movement needs a joint of at least 14 mm; adding manufacturing and installation tolerances, 15–20 mm is chosen. The movement capability is on the sealant data sheet (ISO 11600 class, e.g. 25LM).

Depth and backer rod

Sealant depth should be about half the width (at least 6 mm); a sealant that is too deep restricts movement. A closed-cell backer rod is placed behind it, and the sealant should bond to two sides only; three-sided adhesion causes cracking.
