How to choose glass thickness

Glass thickness is calculated from the wind load and pane size, not chosen "by experience". This guide explains the logic and the points to watch.

  • Inputs: wind load, pane size, support type and glass type.
  • Two checks are made: stress (the glass must not break) and deflection (it must not flex too much).
  • In insulating glass the load is shared between the panes; the interlayer contribution in laminated glass is limited.

1. Load

Wind load is found according to EN 1991-1-4; suction is highest in corner zones. For insulating glass a climatic load (the cavity air expanding with temperature and pressure differences) is added; on small, narrow panes this load can govern.

2. Support type and size

Glass supported on four edges has much lower stress and deflection than glass supported on two edges. The short edge is the governing dimension; as the aspect ratio grows, the pane behaves more like a two-edge supported plate. Point-fixed (spider) glass has stress concentrations around the holes and requires finite element analysis.

3. Strength

Glass typeCharacteristic bending strength
Float (annealed)45 MPa
Heat strengthened70 MPa
Toughened120 MPa

The design strength of float glass depends on load duration: high for short loads such as wind, low for permanent loads such as self-weight (EN 16612, DIN 18008).

4. Deflection

Even when the stress is acceptable, the glass may flex too much. To protect the edge seal of insulating glass and the appearance, specifications usually require a deflection limit such as 1/60–1/100 of the short edge.

5. Laminated and insulating glass

In laminated glass the shear transfer of the PVB interlayer depends on temperature and load duration; to stay on the safe side the interlayer contribution is often taken as low. In insulating glass the outer and inner panes share the load by their stiffness; the thicker pane takes more.
