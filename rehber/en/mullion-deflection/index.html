How is mullion deflection calculated?

The first check when choosing a curtain wall profile is how much the mullion bends under wind. Here is the formula and the limits, with an example.

  • Load on the mullion = wind pressure × mullion spacing.
  • For a simply supported beam δ = 5·q·L⁴ / (384·E·I).
  • EN 13830 limit: L/200 for L ≤ 3 m, 5 mm + L/300 for 3–7.5 m, L/250 above.

Load

A mullion carries half of the glass on each side, so its load width is the mullion spacing. With 1.2 kN/m² wind and 1.5 m spacing, q = 1.8 kN/m (= 1.8 N/mm). The deflection check uses the characteristic (unfactored) load.

Formula

δ = 5 · q · L⁴ / (384 · E · I)

E: 70,000 MPa for aluminium, 210,000 MPa for steel. I: the profile's moment of inertia in the wind direction (mm⁴), taken from the catalogue or CAD. Example: q = 1.8 N/mm, L = 3600 mm, I = 4,000,000 mm⁴ in aluminium gives δ ≈ 14.1 mm. The limit is 5 + 3600/300 = 17 mm, so it passes.

Support conditions

If the mullion hangs from a bracket at each floor and is connected to the next one with a sliding joint at the bottom, a simply supported beam is assumed. Continuous mullions spanning two bays deflect less but have a higher moment at the intermediate support; cantilever solutions need a separate calculation.

What if it isn't enough?

  • Choose a deeper profile (the moment of inertia grows with the cube of the depth).
  • Add steel reinforcement inside the profile: the stiffnesses add up, E·Ial + E·Isteel.
  • Reduce the mullion spacing or add an intermediate support.

Besides deflection, bending stress, bracket and anchor loads and the transom-mullion connection must also be checked.
