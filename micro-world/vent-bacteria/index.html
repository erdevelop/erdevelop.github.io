How does it live?

Mineral-rich water at hundreds of degrees gushes from vents on the ocean floor. The bacteria and archaea here make food from carbon dioxide using the energy of substances such as hydrogen sulfide, methane or hydrogen instead of light; this is called chemosynthesis.

These microbes cover the rocks in thick mats and feed the entire food chain of animals such as tube worms, mussels and shrimp. Some live as partners inside the animals' bodies.

Fun facts

  • Hydrothermal vents were discovered in 1977; an ecosystem without light amazed biologists.
  • Some archaea can multiply even above 120 °C.
  • Some scientists think life may first have begun near vents like these.

Game vs. reality

  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).
  • In the game: In the game you pick evolution cards while alive.
    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
