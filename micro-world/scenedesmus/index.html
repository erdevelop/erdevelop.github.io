How does it live?

A Scenedesmus colony is usually 4 or 8 oval cells lined up side by side, with a spine at the tips of the edge cells.

Colony shape can change with the environment: when it senses the chemical trace of grazers such as water fleas, it builds larger colonies that are harder to eat.

Fun facts

  • Its change of shape in response to grazers is one of the best-known examples of "defensive flexibility" in single-celled organisms.
  • It is used in wastewater treatment and biofuel research.
  • It is a major source of the green colour of lake water.

Game vs. reality

  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).
  • In the game: In the game you pick evolution cards while alive.
    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
