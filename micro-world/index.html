Micro World Encyclopedia

Hundreds of organisms live in a single drop of pond water: single cells that hunt, flee, make food from light or build houses of glass, and animals smaller than a millimetre. These pages tell the real-life stories of the organisms in the game Organizma.

Differences between the game and reality

Organizma is a game: we changed some things to make it fun and brought others as close to reality as we could.

  • True to life: the game world is an estuary (river mouth): fresh water in the middle, brackish water further out and the sea beyond. Freshwater organisms (amoeba, paramecium, euglena, stentor, hydra, water flea) live in the middle, marine ones (radiolarians, silicoflagellates, foraminifera, coccolithophores, Noctiluca) further out; a cell in the wrong water loses or gains water through osmosis.
  • True to life: the pulse in the life panel is not a heartbeat but the contractile vacuole of freshwater cells pumping out excess water: it almost stops in salt water, speeds up in warmth and is absent in marine organisms.
  • True to life: day and night follow the real clock (Istanbul time): algae grow in daylight, zooplankton migrate to the depths at dawn, oxygen runs low where dead cells decay, cells can absorb algae (endosymbiosis), form cysts and swap genes by conjugation.
  • True to life: viruses cannot swim and drift with the water; Didinium hunts alone; the giants are real organisms (the giant amoeba Chaos, an amoeba infected by Mimivirus, the sac-shaped giant ciliate Bursaria).
  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).
  • In the game: In the game you pick evolution cards while alive.
    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
  • In the game: In the game you dash forward.
    In reality: Some ciliates really make sudden escape jumps, but not on demand with a cooldown.
  • In the game: In the game cells burp and show emotes.
    In reality: Purely for fun: cells have no voice or feelings.
  • In the game: there are specialisation paths (a long-range venom dart, summoning helper cells, a protective shield).
    In reality: these are game inventions inspired by real abilities (the venom darts of Dileptus, colonial life, symbiosis).

Look for yourself with a microscope

You can see most of these organisms with a simple school microscope. Put some pond water in a jar with a little mud from the bottom and some water plants, and leave it somewhere bright for a few days. Put a drop on a slide and look at 40–100× magnification: first you will see paramecia, euglenas and diatoms, and with patience rotifers, vorticellas and, if you are lucky, a tardigrade.
