Radiolarian · Radiolaria

An elegant marine single cell with a glass skeleton that lasts for millions of years on the ocean floor.

How does it live?

A radiolarian lives inside an extremely ornate skeleton made of opal-like silica (glass). Fine rays reaching out from the skeleton both catch prey and help the cell float.

In many species, small photosynthetic algae live in the cytoplasm; the radiolarian gives them shelter and the algae give it food.

When the cell dies its skeleton slowly sinks to the bottom. In some parts of the ocean most of the floor is radiolarian ooze made of these skeletons.

Fun facts

  • The most famous plates in German biologist Ernst Haeckel's 1904 book Kunstformen der Natur (Art Forms in Nature) are radiolarians.
  • Their fossils go back to the Cambrian, about 500 million years ago, and help geologists date rocks.
  • Each of the thousands of known species has its own skeleton shape, so species are often told apart by their skeleton alone.

Game vs. reality

  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).
  • In the game: In the game you pick evolution cards while alive.
    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
