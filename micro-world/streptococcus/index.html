How does it live?

Streptococci break down sugars without oxygen and produce lactic acid. Streptococcus thermophilus, which turns milk into yoghurt, is one of them.

Because the cells always divide in the same direction and stay attached, they look like a string of pearls under the microscope. Some members of the group can cause illnesses such as strep throat.

Fun facts

  • One of the two bacteria in yoghurt starter is a streptococcus (the other is a rod from the genus Lactobacillus).
  • The name comes from the Greek streptos, "twisted chain".
  • Streptococcus mutans in our mouths makes acid from sugar and contributes to tooth decay.

Game vs. reality

  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).
  • In the game: In the game you pick evolution cards while alive.
    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
