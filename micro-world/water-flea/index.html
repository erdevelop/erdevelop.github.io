How does it live?

The body of a water flea sits inside an almost transparent shell; under the microscope you can clearly see its heart beating, its gut filling with green algae and eggs developing in the brood pouch on its back.

Fine hairs on its legs keep water flowing and filter it; water fleas can filter a large share of a pond's water in a single day. That is why they play a big part in keeping water clear.

In good conditions females produce only female young without mating. When the water cools and food runs short, males appear and tough ephippium eggs are formed that can survive drought and freezing.

Fun facts

  • They are a standard test animal in water quality and toxicity tests: their sensitivity gives early warning of pollution.
  • They are widely used as fish food in aquariums.
  • When they smell a predator, some species grow a "helmet" on their head or a long spine on their tail.

Game vs. reality

  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).
  • In the game: In the game you pick evolution cards while alive.
    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
