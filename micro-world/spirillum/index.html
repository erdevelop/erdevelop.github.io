Spirillum (spiral bacterium) · Spirillum volutans

How does it live?

The body of a spirillum is rigid and helical. When the flagella tufts at both ends spin together, the cell "screws" itself through the water like a corkscrew; it reverses them to change direction.

It likes neither oxygen-rich nor oxygen-free water and seeks the low-oxygen layer. In a drop of water they often gather in a band near the oxygen boundary.

Fun facts

  • Spirillum volutans can reach 60 µm, dozens of times longer than most bacteria, and is easy to see with a light microscope.
  • Spiral bacteria come in three shapes: rigid spirilla, flexible spirochetes and comma-shaped vibrios.
  • In the 1680s Antoni van Leeuwenhoek was among the first to see spiral bacteria, in samples from between his teeth.

Game vs. reality

  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).

    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
