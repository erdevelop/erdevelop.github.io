Heliozoan (sun animalcule) · Actinosphaerium and Actinophrys

Erdal Üstünol · Nov 26, 2026 · Micro World Encyclopedia

A single cell that looks like a little sun with thin rays in every direction, and catches its prey with those rays.

How does it live?

Thin, stiff rays called axopods stick out in every direction from its round body. Inside each ray, bundles of microtubules act as a skeleton.

A small organism that bumps into the rays gets stuck on tiny sticky, toxic granules on their surface. The ray shortens and carries the prey to the body, which wraps it into a food vacuole. Several heliozoans can even fuse to digest a large prey together.

Fun facts

  • The rays can be taken apart and rebuilt within minutes; they are a classic example of how dynamic the cell skeleton is.
  • Despite its name it does not feed on light; the "sun" refers only to its looks.

Game vs. reality

  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).
  • In the game: In the game you pick evolution cards while alive.
    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
