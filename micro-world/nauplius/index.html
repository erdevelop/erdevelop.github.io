Nauplius larva · Nauplius stage of crustaceans

The tiny one-eyed, three-pairs-of-limbs larva that hatches from the eggs of copepods, shrimp and barnacles.

How does it live?

The nauplius is a pear-shaped larva whose body is not yet segmented. In the middle of its head is a single naupliar eye, and it has three pairs of limbs it uses as oars.

In copepods the hatchling goes through six nauplius stages, moulting and growing a little each time, then through copepodite stages to adulthood.

Fun facts

  • Crustaceans with very different-looking adults (shrimp, copepods, barnacles) all start from the same kind of larva; their kinship was first recognised from nauplius similarity.
  • Fish farms raise brine shrimp (Artemia) nauplii as food for young fish.
  • They are among the most numerous animal larvae in the ocean.

Game vs. reality

  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).
  • In the game: In the game you pick evolution cards while alive.
    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
