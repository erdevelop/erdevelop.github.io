Didinium · Didinium nasutum

Erdal Üstünol · Nov 26, 2026 · Micro World Encyclopedia

A barrel-shaped hunter that catches paramecia bigger than itself with a toxic snout and swallows them whole.

How does it live?

Didinium carries a proboscis at the front of its barrel-shaped body. When it hits its prey, toxicysts in the proboscis paralyse the victim and stick the two cells together.

It then opens its mouth astonishingly wide and swallows a paramecium larger than itself whole within a few minutes. A single didinium can eat several paramecia a day and divides quickly once fed.

When food runs out it turns into a thick-walled cyst and waits until paramecia multiply again.

Fun facts

  • In 1934 the Russian biologist G. F. Gause put didinium and paramecium in the same tube and ran one of the first laboratory experiments on predator–prey relationships.
  • In a simple tube the predator eats all its prey and then starves; but when a refuge and new immigrants are added, the two species live together and their populations oscillate.
  • It is one of the few single cells that can swallow a cell bigger than itself.

In the game Organizma

The red hunters in Organizma are didinia: as in reality they hunt alone and go after growing cells. The occasional Didinium outbreak event is inspired by Gause's predator–prey cycle: when prey is plentiful, predators multiply too.

Game vs. reality

  • In the game: In the game didinium bites chunks off big cells.
    In reality: In reality it paralyses its prey with its proboscis, swallows it whole and feeds almost only on paramecia.
  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).
  • In the game: In the game you pick evolution cards while alive.
    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
