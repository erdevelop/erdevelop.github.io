Mimivirus (giant virus) · Acanthamoeba polyphaga mimivirus

Erdal Üstünol · Nov 26, 2026 · Micro World Encyclopedia

A giant virus big enough to see with a light microscope, once mistaken for a bacterium; it multiplies inside amoebae.

How does it live?

Mimivirus was found in 1992 inside amoebae from a cooling tower and was long thought to be a bacterium. When it was recognised as a virus in 2003, it was the largest virus known.

The amoeba swallows it, taking it for food. Inside, the virus builds a large virus factory that copies its genetic material; when hundreds of new viruses have formed, the amoeba bursts.

Fun facts

  • Its genome has over a million base pairs and around a thousand genes, more than some bacteria.
  • Its name means "mimicking microbe".
  • A smaller virus called Sputnik "sneaks into" the mimivirus factory to multiply: even viruses have viruses.

Game vs. reality

  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).
  • In the game: In the game you pick evolution cards while alive.
    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
