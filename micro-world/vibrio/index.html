How does it live?

Vibrios are slightly curved, comma-like cells. By spinning their single flagellum very fast they become some of the fastest swimmers among bacteria.

Most live in seawater and estuaries, clinging to the chitin shells of copepods and other crustaceans. Some species glow; a few cause disease in humans.

Fun facts

  • Cholera is caused by Vibrio cholerae; John Snow's 1854 linking of a London outbreak to a water pump is seen as the birth of epidemiology.
  • Vibrio fischeri lives in the light organ of the Hawaiian bobtail squid and makes the light that gives it night-time "invisibility".
  • Bacteria "talking" to each other with chemical signals (quorum sensing) was first discovered in glowing vibrios.

Game vs. reality

  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).
  • In the game: In the game you pick evolution cards while alive.
    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
