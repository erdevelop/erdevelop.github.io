Rotifer (wheel animal) · Rotifera

Erdal Üstünol · Nov 26, 2026 · Micro World Encyclopedia

A tiny animal whose ring of cilia looks like a spinning wheel, and which can survive frozen for thousands of years.

How does it live?

On the head of a rotifer are two rings of cilia called the corona, which look like a turning wheel. These cilia both make the animal swim and sweep food towards its mouth.

Behind the mouth, a muscular jaw apparatus called the mastax grinds the food. Although its body has only about a thousand cells, it has real organs such as a brain, a digestive system and an excretory system.

Fun facts

  • Bdelloid rotifers have reproduced without males, with females only, for tens of millions of years.
  • In 2021 rotifers taken from Siberian soil that had been frozen for 24,000 years were reported to revive and reproduce.
  • When it dries out it can lose almost all its water and go into a "dry sleep", coming back to life when wetted.

Game vs. reality

  • In the game: In the game organisms collect the genes of what they eat.
    In reality: In reality genes are mostly passed from parent to offspring. Close parallels to getting genes from food are rare: horizontal gene transfer in bacteria, and a cell turning a swallowed alga into an organelle (endosymbiosis; this is how mitochondria and chloroplasts arose).
  • In the game: In the game you pick evolution cards while alive.
    In reality: In reality an individual does not evolve during its life; mutations build up from generation to generation. The game's generation inheritance is therefore closer to reality.
